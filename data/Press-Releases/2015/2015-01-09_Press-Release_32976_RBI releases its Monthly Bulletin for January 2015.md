## January 9, 2015

## RBI releases its Monthly Bulletin for January 2015

The Reserve Bank of India today released the <u>January 2015</u> issue of its monthly Bulletin. Apart from regular features like speeches of the Top Management and Current Statistics, the Bulletin includes two special articles: 1. <u>India’s Foreign Trade: 2014-15</u> <u>(April-September)</u> and 2. <u>Finances of Foreign Direct Investment Companies, 2012-13</u>.

## 1. India’s Foreign Trade: 2014-15

India’s trade performance improved significantly in Q1 of 2014-15. However, this was reversed in Q2 of 2014-15. Major highlights of India’s trade performance during April-September 2014 are set out below:

## Highlights:

- Export growth increased from 6.3 per cent in April-September 2013 to 6.5 per cent in April-September 2014.
- Export growth was mainly contributed by a few sectors, *viz*., engineering goods, petroleum products, readymade garments and chemicals.
- Exports to the US, the UAE, China and South Korea gained momentum, but decelerated or turned negative in respect of Saudi Arabia, Hong Kong, Singapore and most EU countries.
- With the increase in both oil and non-oil non-gold imports, India’s imports grew by
1.6 per cent during April-September 2014 as against a decline of 2.5 per cent during April-September 2013.
- Non-oil non-gold imports have shown an upturn since May 2014.
- While India’s imports from the EU, the US, Switzerland, Iraq, Kuwait, Saudi Arabia and UAE have moderated, those from China picked up.
- Despite the import growth turning modestly positive and export growth rising marginally, India’s trade deficit narrowed to US$ 70.4 billion during April- September 2014 as compared with US$ 76.7 billion during April-September 2013.
## 2. Finances of Foreign Direct Investment Companies, 2012-13

This article presents the financial performance of non-government non-financial foreign direct investment companies during the year 2012-13 based on the audited annual accounts of select 917 FDI companies. The study also presents a comparable picture over a three year period from 2010-11 to 2012-13 based on these common companies’ data.

<!-- PAGE 2 -->

## Major findings:

- The sales growth of select FDI companies along with their value of production and operating expenses moderated in 2012-13. The sales growth was lower at 14.2 per cent in 2012-13 as compared with 17.7 per cent growth recorded in 2011-12.
- The earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation (EBITDA) and net profits (PAT) improved in 2012-13 mainly due to a moderation in manufacturing expenses and interest expenses, respectively.
- The growth in total net assets of select FDI companies decelerated during the year and the share of gross fixed assets formation in total assets had improved in 2012-13. The total net assets of select FDI companies grew at a lower rate of 11.1 per cent in 2012-13 *vis-à-vis* 13.4 per cent registered in the previous year.
- The share of long-term and short-term borrowings in total liabilities increased in 2012-13 leading to rise in the leverage ratio of select FDI companies during the year.
- The profitability ratios like EBITDA margin and return on equity (ROE) declined in 2012-13 mainly as a result of elevated leverage ratio.
- The select FDI companies relied mainly on external sources of funds for expanding their business and these funds were predominantly used for gross fixed assets formation and long-term investments. The detailed data pertaining to the Finances of the Foreign Direct Investment
Companies for 2012-13 was released on the RBI website on December 26, 2014 vide <u>press release No.1332</u>
