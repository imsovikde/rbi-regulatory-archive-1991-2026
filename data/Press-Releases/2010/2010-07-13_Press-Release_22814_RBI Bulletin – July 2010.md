## July 13, 2010

## RBI Bulletin – July 2010

The July issue of the <u>Reserve Bank of India Bulletin</u> includes four articles.

**Foreign Trade: April 2010** This article reviews India’s merchandise trade performance during April 2010 on the basis of data released by the Ministry of Commerce and Industry and Directorate General of Commercial Intelligence and Statistics (DGCI&S). The article also covers disaggregated commodity-wise and direction-wise analysis of India’s trade during the period April-November 2009.

## Main Findings

The article observes that during April 2010, India’s merchandise exports at US$

16.9 billion posted a growth of 36.2 per cent over the corresponding month of previous year as against a decline of 32.8 per cent during April 2009. Merchandise imports at US$ 27.3 billion recorded a growth of 43.3 per cent over the corresponding month of previous year as against a decline of 37.2 per cent during April 2009. The oil and non- oil imports of India also registered a growth of 70.5 per cent and 34.3 per cent in April 2010, respectively, as against a decline of 46.9 per cent and 33.1 per cent in April
2009. Further, the disaggregated data on commodity-wise merchandise trade as well as source and direction of exports and imports reveal that trade of all major commodity groups (*viz.*, primary products, manufactured goods, petroleum products and bulk and non bulk import items) and with major trading partners (*viz.*, OECD, OPEC, Eastern Europe and Developing Countries) registered a decline during April-November 2009 over the corresponding period of previous year.
## Indian Investment Abroad in Joint Ventures and Wholly Owned

**Subsidiaries: 2009-10 (April-March)** reviews India’s outward foreign direct investment (FDI) in joint ventures (JVs) and wholly owned subsidiaries (WOSs) during the quarter January-March 2010 and during 2009-10 (April-March).

## Main Findings

The article observes that during the quarter under review, India’s outward investment stood at US$ 1.9 billion, showing a decline of 52.7 per cent over the corresponding quarter of the previous year. During the quarter under review, there was an increase in outflows financed through loans while outflows financed through equity experienced a sharp decline. During 2009-10, the actual outward FDI in JVs and WOSs stood at US$ 10.3 billion, which was 36.5 per cent lower than the investment made during the previous year. During 2009-10, the investment financed through loans registered a growth over the previous year whereas the investment financed through equity declined sharply.

Activity-wise, manufacturing sector occupied the largest share in India’s outward investment during last quarter of 2009-10 and during 2009-10 as a whole. However, the share of manufacturing declined during 2009-10 in comparison to previous year. Direction-wise, a larger part of India’s outward investment was made in Mauritius during

**India's External Debt as at end-March 2010** reviews India’s external debt during the full year 2009-10 (April-March).

## Main Findings

The article on India’s External Debt places India’s external debt, as at end-March 2010, at US$ 261.4 billion or 18.9 per cent of GDP. The long-term debt at US$ 209.0 billion and short-term debt at US$ 52.4 billion accounted for 79.9 per cent and 20.1 per cent, respectively, of the total external debt. The article also provides tables and charts on various details on external debt like External Debt Outstanding, External Debt by Component, Currency Composition, Instrument-wise classification, Short-Term Debt by Original Maturity and Residual Maturity etc. A detailed account of external debt as compiled in the standard format as at end-March 2010 in Rupee and US Dollar terms and revised data for the earlier quarters are provided in Statement 1 and 2, respectively.

## Developments in India's Balance of Payments during the Fourth Quarter

**(January-March 2010) of 2009-10 and full Year 2009-10** reviews India’s Balance of Payments (BoP) for the quarter January-March 2010 and also for the full year 2009-10 (April-March)

## Main Findings

The higher trade deficit combined with the lower invisibles surplus widened the current account deficit during Q4 of 2009-10. After four consecutive quarters of decline, on a year-on-year basis, services receipts recorded a growth of 13.4 per cent led by software and financial services. Private transfer receipts continued to be robust during Q4 of 2009-10. The overall balance was in surplus at US$ 2.1 billion, which resulted in a net accretion to foreign exchange reserves of equivalent amount.

During 2009-10, despite a lower trade deficit on BoP basis, decline in invisibles surplus led to higher current account deficit at US$ 38.4 billion (2.9 per cent of GDP) during 2009-10, as compared with US$ 28.7 billion (2.4 per cent of GDP) during 2008-

09. The surplus in the capital account increased sharply to US$ 53.6 billion (4.1 per cent of GDP) during the year from US$ 7.3 billion (0.6 per cent of GDP) a year ago. As the surplus in the capital account exceeded the current account deficit, there was a net accretion to foreign exchange reserves of US$ 13.4 billion during 2009-10 on BoP basis
i.e. excluding valuation effects (as against a drawdown of reserves of US$ 20.1 billion during 2008-09). The article provides details with tables and charts on key components of invisibles, movements in current account balance, capital flows as well as variation in reserves. Statement-I presents preliminary data for Q4 and partially revised data for the earlier quarters of 2009-10, based on which BoP data for the full year 2009-10 have been compiled.
