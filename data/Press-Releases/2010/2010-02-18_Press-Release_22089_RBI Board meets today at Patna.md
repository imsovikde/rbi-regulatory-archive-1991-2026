## February 18, 2010

## RBI Board meets today at Patna

The Central Board of the Reserve Bank of India met today in Patna after a span of six years. The meeting reviewed key economic, monetary and financial developments.

Dr D. Subbarao, Governor, Reserve Bank of India chaired the Central Board meeting. Shri Y.H. Malegam, Prof. Suresh Tendulkar, Prof. U. R. Rao, Shri Lakshmi Chand, Shri H.P. Ranina, Smt. Shashi Rajagopalan, Shri Suresh Neotia, Dr. A. Vaidyanathan, Prof M. M. Sharma and Shri Sanjay Labroo were present in the meeting of the Central Board. Deputy Governors of the Reserve Bank, Smt. Shyamala Gopinath, Smt. Usha Thorat, Dr. K.C. Chakrabarty and Dr. Subir Gokarn were also present.

The Central Board of Directors of the Reserve Bank meets at least once every quarter. The Board has scheduled meetings in Mumbai, Chennai and Kolkata each year. The post-budget meeting of the Board, traditionally held in New Delhi, is addressed by the Union Finance Minister. The other meetings of the Board are held in the remaining state capitals by rotation. The main function of the Central Board of the Reserve Bank is to provide overall direction to its affairs.

After the meeting of the Central Board, the Reserve Bank Governor met with senior officials of the State Government and controlling heads of banks represented in Bihar. The meeting focused on banking related issues, such as, increasing credit- deposit ratio, increasing banking presence in under-banked areas through branch expansion as well as Banking Correspondent/Banking facilitator (BF/BC) model, problem of non-lending/low lending by private banks in Bihar and the issue of transparency in disposal of loan applications.

Earlier, the Reserve Bank Governor called on Shri Nitish Kumar, the Hon’ble Chief Minister of Bihar on February17, 2010 and discussed issues of mutual interest, like inflation conditions, credit-deposit ratio and problems in credit dispensation and branch expansion. Aspects related to the legislative hurdles in mortgaging agricultural land for commercial purpose, development of the self help group (SHG) movement and introduction of electronic benefit transfer in Bihar were also discussed.
