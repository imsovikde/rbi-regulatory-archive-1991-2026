# RBI Booklet on Y2K

The Reserve Bank of India has printed a booklet on "Year 2000 Preparations in the Banking and Financial Sector". The publication brings out clearly the broad approach adopted by banks and financial institutions in tackling the problem of Year 2000.

The Reserve Bank of India initiated a process of creation of awareness in banks and financial institutions way back in September 1996 when a detailed circular was issued advising the banks to prepare a detailed inventory of the hardware, operating system software and application software that they possess and do the scoping and diagnostics to assess whether the systems are Y2K compliant or not.

This was followed by making available to banks and financial institutions material on the subject received from a number to sources including the Bank for International Settlement, the Bank of England, the FED, Presentations were organised by Indian Banks' Association (IBA) and the Reserve Bank of India through National Institute for Bank Management (NIBM), Tata Consultancy Services, Tata Infotech, Standard Chartered Bank, ANZ Grindlays, Citibank and State Bank of India. Test kits were made available to the banks and financial institutions so that they could test each and every computer. They were also advised to repair, replace or abandon machines depending on the test results by Y2K version of the machines. The major MICR cheque processing systems at the four metropolitan centres operated by the Reserve Bank of India which are at present not Y2K compliant, are being replaced and will be Y2K compliant.

The Reserve Bank of India has constituted a high level committee under its Deputy Governor (Shri S.P.Talwar) to monitor bimonthly the progress made by the Reserve Bank of India, banks and financial institutions, non-banking financial companies in the areas of Y2K problem. The other members of the committee are three Executive Directors of the Reserve Bank, Chief General Mangers of regulatory and supervisory departments of the Reserve Bank, IBA, NIBM and few selected banks. Representatives of Ministry of Finance and Department of Electronics are also invited to the Committee.

Banks and financial institutions are required to monitor the progress through a core group set up in each bank. The Core Groups report to regional/zonal Offices and the zonal offices in turn report to top managements of banks. The boards of directors of each bank and financial institutions would review the progress bimonthly. A bimonthly report is submitted by them to the Reserve Bank of India.

As per the reports available, over 70 per cent of the Commercial Banks and their subsidiaries are expected to be Y2K compliant by December 1998.

## (Alpana Killawala) General Manager
