The approved Plan outlay of the States in 1997-98 has been placed at Rs.62,732 crore, excluding Sikkim, of which, 44.5 per cent is estimated to be financed by Central assistance and 11.4 per cent through market borrowings. Many States have negative balance from current revenue (BCR), which affect the size of States' Plans. The negative contribution from public sector undertakings like State Electricity Board, Road Transport Corporations have also affected the States' Plan size.

The stress on the finances of State Governments has been evident since the `eighties. The steady deterioration in the revenue account caused enlargement in the gross fiscal deficit of the States. The gross fiscal deficit of the States has been stagnant around 3.0 per cent of GDP since the mid-eighties. The financing of the gross fiscal deficit through borrowings has created an enormous debt-servicing burden.

The public sector undertakings of the States, particularly State Electricity Boards and State Road Transport Corporations, have persistently recorded losses and therefore their contribution to the State exchequer is negative. Several irrigation projects have also contributed negatively to the State budgets. The rising losses recorded by the State public sector undertakings have put pressure on the optimal allocation of resources, through the budget, as a large proportion of resources is diverted towards current expenditure leading to a decline in capital expenditure-GDP ratio.

A sustained improvement in State finances could be achieved only through reduction in the borrowing requirements of the States. The improvement in gross fiscal deficit should be achieved through the revenue account. To contain the revenue gap from widening further it would be necessary to enlarge the tax base which would augment resources to absorb the rising expenditure. This would require, therefore, fresh tax and non-tax measures, and also the need to rationalise and reform the existing tax structure for better compliance. States should give high priority for the development of human capital through expansion of social and economic infrastructure like education, basic health, drinking water, etc. In this context, States need to encourage private sector participation in the development of social and economic infrastructure which would reduce stress on States' resources. The additional resources which would be made available to States out of Voluntary Disclosure of Income Scheme (VDIS) in 1998-99 should be utilised for the development of infrastructure facilities. Further, States need to focus on the issue of subsidies, user charges, pricing policies and better management of public sector undertakings.
