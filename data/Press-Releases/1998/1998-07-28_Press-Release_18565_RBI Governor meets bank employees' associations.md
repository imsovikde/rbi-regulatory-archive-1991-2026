Welcoming the representatives of the all India employees' associations, Governor Dr. Bimal Jalan expressed his high appreciation of the visit paid by the national level leaders of bank employees to the Reserve Bank. The visit assumed significance as its main objective was exchange of views on issues concerning the future of the banking and financial sector.

In his introductory remarks, the Governor stated that the Indian banking sector had an important role to play in a changing global environment. He added that the banking sector in India is expected to make significant contribution to India's economic growth and in increasing employment opportunities. It also plays an important in making India's external sector strong. The Governor emphasised that the Indian banking sector would be strong and vibrant only if the management, officers and the staff of the banks made joint efforts towards achieving this objective. He appreciated the cooperation extended by all sections of the staff in the banking industry in keeping the industrial scenario purposeful and constructive. He was confident that this tradition will continue in the years to come.

Stating that the Indian banking industry had many achieve ments to its credit, the Governor specifically referred to the extension of banking services to the poorest through widening of its branch network. He then drew the attention of the associations that some areas in which the banking industry could do more were customer service and information technology. He said that banks needed to expeditiously reduce the transaction time and costs in order to improve their service and efficiency.

The representatives of the all India employees' associations reiterated their cooperation for the future growth of the banking industry so as to ensure the country's development. The representatives, however, expressed their concerns about some of the views detailed in the Narasimham Committee on Banking Sector Reforms.

In this connection, they reiterated their views expressed in their memorandum to the Government of India that some of the structural recommendations were not in the interest of the bank ing industry. They reaffirmed their strong support for public sector banks remaining in the public sector and said that unions were not in favour of reducing government holding in public sector banks to 33 per cent as recommended by the Narasimham Committee. With regard to weak banks, it was pointed out that several of these banks provided important services in different parts of the country and any narrowing of their functions will not be in the country's interest.

The asset reconstruction fund which was suggested by the Narasimham Committee, according to the representatives of the employees' associations, was, among other things, fraught with moral hazard. There was also a discussion of the legal and other measures needed to effect quick recovery of loans and reducing non-performing assets of the banks. A view was expressed that this can only be done if banks have the power of attaching the property and defaulting borrowers are penalised effectively. It was also suggested that the Reserve Bank should publish the information on defaulting borrowers even if court cases have not been filed. Among other matters which came up for discussion were:the need for providing suitable safeguards to banking personnel involved in decision making, pros and cons of induction of new technology in the banking services and the need to strengthen management-employee cooperation and consultations at the industry level.

<!-- PAGE 3 -->

The Governor noted the valuable suggestions made by the associations and assured that the Reserve Bank would take them fully into account while formulating its own views with regard to the future growth of the banking industry.

(Alpana Killawala) **General Manager**
