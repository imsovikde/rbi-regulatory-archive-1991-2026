# RBI Issues Guidelines For "When Issued" market in Central Government Securities

` PRESS RELEASE

RESERVE BANK OF INDIA www.rbi.org.in PRESS RELATIONS DIVISION, Central Office, Post Box 406, Mumbai 400001 <u>www.rbi.org.in</u> \hindi Phone: 2266 0502 Fax: 2266 0358, 2270 3279 e-mail: helpprd@rbi.org.in May 3, 2006

RBI Issues Guidelines For "When Issued" market in Central Government Securities

The Reserve Bank of India has today issued guidelines on trading in Central Government Securities on 'When Issued' (WI) basis by select participants.

'When Issued', a short form of "when as and if issued", indicates a conditional transaction in a security authorised for issuance but not as yet actually issued. All 'When Issued' transactions are on an "if" basis, to be settled if and when the actual security is issued. 'When Issued' market has two basic advantages:

(i) It facilitates the distribution process for Government securities by stretching the actual distribution period for each issue and allowing the market more time to absorb large issues without disruption; and (ii) It also facilitates price discovery process by reducing uncertainties surrounding auctions. Salient features of the guidelines issued today are:
a. To start with 'WI' trades will be permitted in case of only reissued securities.
b. Only Primary Dealers will be allowed to take short positions in 'WI' securities, and every 'WI' transaction will require at least one Primary Dealer as counterparty.
c. Participants will be able to undertake 'WI' positions within prescribed limits. Prescribed limits on 'WI' positions are:
i) Primary Dealer : 10% (long or short positions) ii) Non Primary Dealers : 5% long positions
d. Internal control and reporting requirements have been prescribed for effective control and regulation of 'WI' market.
## Detailed guidelines are available on www.rbi.org.in

It may be noted that under the provisions of Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management (FRBM) Act, 2003, the Reserve Bank does not participate in the primary issuance process of Central Government Securities effective April 1, 2006. This stipulation has fundamentally altered the paradigm of debt management function of the Reserve Bank. This has necessitated a comprehensive restructuring of the debt issuance framework with a view to ensuring that while the debt issuance process remained entirely market determined, the Reserve Bank's debt management objectives continue to be achieved. The Internal Technical Group on Central Government Securities of the Reserve Bank constituted to examine the issues in the light of FRBM Act had recommended, among other things, introduction of 'When Issued' market in Central Government Securities.

Actual trading on ‘When Issued’ basis would commence from a date to be indicated by the Reserve Bank, after necessary infrastructure is in place.
