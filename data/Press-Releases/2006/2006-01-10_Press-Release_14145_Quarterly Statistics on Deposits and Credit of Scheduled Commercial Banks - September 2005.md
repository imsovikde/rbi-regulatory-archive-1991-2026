## January 10, 2006

“Quarterly Statistics on Deposits and Credit of <u>Scheduled Commercial Banks”: September 2005</u>

“Quarterly Statistics on Deposits and Credit of Scheduled Commercial Banks-September 2005” provides data on aggregate deposits and gross bank credit of scheduled commercial banks as on last Friday of September 2005. The data are based on Basic Statistical Return-7, received from the branches of all scheduled commercial banks (including Regional Rural Banks). During this quarter, there was amalgamation of 28 RRBs into 9 RRBs leading to reduction in number of RRBs from 196 to 177.

The number of banked centres served by scheduled commercial banks according to number of reporting offices stood at 34,747. Of these centres, 29,303 were single office centres and 40 centers had 100 or more bank offices.

The top hundred centers, out of 34,747 banked centers, arranged according to the size of deposits accounted for 66.2 per cent of the total deposits and the top hundred centres arranged according to the size of bank credit accounted for 75.9 per cent of total bank credit. Aggregate deposits of top hundred centres grew at 24.2 per cent in September 2005 over September 2004 compared to 19.6 per cent growth recorded a year ago. Annual growth rate of gross bank credit of top hundred centres was appreciably higher at 37.8 per cent (including the impact of IDBI Ltd.) in September 2005 over September 2004, compared to 24.9 per cent growth recorded in September 2004.

Nationalised Banks, as a group, accounted for 49.6 per cent of the aggregate deposits, while State Bank of India and its Associates accounted for 24.6 per cent. The shares of Foreign Banks, Regional Rural Banks and Other Scheduled Commercial Banks in aggregate deposits were 4.9 per cent, 3.3 per cent and 17.7 per cent, respectively. As regards gross bank credit, Nationalised Banks held the maximum share of 47.8 per cent in the total bank credit followed by State Bank of India and its Associates at 23.8 per cent and Other Scheduled Commercial Banks at 18.9 per cent. Foreign Banks and Regional Rural Banks had relatively lower shares in the total bank credit at 6.7 per cent and 2.8 per cent, respectively.

At the all–India level, the credit-deposit (C-D) ratio of all scheduled commercial banks as on the last Friday of September 2005 stood at 68.1 per cent. Among the States/Union Territories, the highest C-D ratio was observed in Tamil Nadu (103.2 per cent), followed by Maharashtra (96.3 per cent) and Chandigarh (94.9 per cent). At the bank group level, the C-D ratio was above the all-India ratio in respect of Foreign Banks

(93.1 per cent) and Other Scheduled Commercial Banks (72.9 per cent), and was lower for State Bank of India and its Associates (65.9 per cent), Nationalised Banks (65.7 per cent) and Regional Rural Banks (57.0 per cent). As regards population group-wise C-D ratio of all scheduled commercial banks, metropolitan centres had the highest C-D ratio at 85.0 per cent, followed by rural centres (54.4 per cent) and urban centres (52.3 per cent). The semi-urban centres recorded the lowest C-D ratio at 46.5 per cent.

It is observed from the distribution of scheduled commercial bank offices by size of deposits, that the offices with deposits of Rs.10 crore or more, accounted for 48.8 per cent of the bank offices with a share of 91.8 per cent in aggregate deposits and 89.1 per cent in total bank credit. The offices, which extended the credit of Rs.10 crore or more, accounted for 23.3 per cent in terms of their number. These offices together accounted for 85.0 per cent of total bank credit whereas their share in aggregate deposits was 64.0 per cent.
