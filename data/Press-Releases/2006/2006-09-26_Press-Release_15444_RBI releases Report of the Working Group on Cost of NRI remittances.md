## September 26, 2006

RBI releases Report of the Working Group on Cost of NRI remittances

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) today placed on its websit<u>e (www.rbi.org.in) the</u> Report of the Working Group on the Cost of Non-Resident Indian (NRI) Remittances. The Report was submitted to the Reserve Bank of India on August 29, 2006. Views / suggestions on the recommendations of the Group may please be sent to the Chief th General Manager-in-Charge, Foreign Exchange Department, 11 floor, Central Office Building, Reserve Bank of India, Fort, Mumbai-400 001 or faxed at 022-2266 1722 or e- mailed at neenajain@rbi.org.in within 15 days.

In the Annual Policy announced in April 2006, it was observed that remittances from NRIs constituted a significant segment of the country's foreign exchange flows but there were concerns on the relatively high cost of remittance faced by the migrants specially for small value remittances. In this context, an External Working Group under the chairmanship of Chief Executive of the Foreign Exchange Dealers' Association of India (FEDAI), with representatives from Bank of Baroda, Citibank N. A., The Hongkong and Shanghai Banking Corporation Ltd., ICICI Bank Limited and State Bank of India was constituted to look into the components that go into pricing of remittances into India. Apart from this, the Group was also asked to compare the price structure with the cost of remittances made out of India and identify elements that result in additional cost.

The Group has made several recommendations; most significant being that banks in India should review their existing scale of charges both at the foreign end and in the domestic centres and resort to latest technology for handling large volume of transactions. Second, banks may improve their infrastructure by extending the scope of existing electronic transfer facilities like the Real Time Gross Settlement (RTGS) or setting up Centralised Remittance Receiving Centres and widening the scope of Exchange Houses. Third, Indian banks should explore tie-ups with more correspondent banks which would bring down the cost for the NRIs at the foreign centres. Fourth, the Group has suggested that the cap on number of branches of Indian banks with drawal arrangements with Exchange Houses may be reviewed. Finally, NRIs may be advised to route their remittances through a branch of an Indian bank or a foreign bank having a branch in India. NRIs should be advised, as far as possible, to convert foreign currencies into Indian Rupees at the Indian end to get the benefit of a better exchange rate. The recommendations of the Group would be examined for such action as deemed necessary to refine the cost and mode of remittance facilities.
