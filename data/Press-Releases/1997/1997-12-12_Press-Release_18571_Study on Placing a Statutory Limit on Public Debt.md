Country experiences show that while many countries have placed limits on government's access to central bank credit, a major step towards limiting debt financing has come from the European Union countries where two separate limits for government borrowings, one based on flow of debt in a year and the other based on the debt stock at the end of every financial year. The limits have been fixed as a ratio to the GDP. There are countries like the United States which aims to move towards a zero deficit goal by the year 2002 and Indonesia which has been practising the balanced budget criteria right since 1966. New Zealand has embarked upon a comprehensive reform towards prudential fiscal management under the new Fiscal Responsibility Act.

Viewed in the background of the analysis of public debt, the rationale for fixing a statutory limit and the other country experiences, the issues and options in the Indian context are discussed in the light of constitutional provisions. Under Article 292 of the Indian Constitution, the Parliament is empowered to fix limit on borrowings upon the security of the Consolidated Fund of India. Under Article 293 of the Indian Constitution, States have similar powers. The important questions to be addressed before fixing a limit on public debt are :

- what should be the level of limit on debt? Should the limit cover only public debt or other liabilities of the Government?
- Should the limit be fixed in absolute terms or as percentage of meaningful denominators like public expenditure or GDP?
While a constitutional amendment would be required for extending the limit to all liabilities of the Government, the initial effort could be to fix limits in terms of both flow and stock taking into account only borrowings secured under the Consolidated Fund of India and mandatorily placing it before the Parliament along with supplementary information on other liabilities including guarantees and the overall public sector deficit. The Government's endeavour in the medium term could be to bring about a suitable constitutional amendment, enhancing the scope of Parliamentary powers to cover all liabilities.
