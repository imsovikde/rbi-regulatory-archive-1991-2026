Dec08,1997

The Reserve Bank of India today announced constitution of a Working Group with a view

to harmonising the role and operations of development finance institutions (DFls) and

banks. Headed by Shri S.H. Khan, Chairman & Managing Director, Industrial

Development Bank of India, the Working Group will have the following terms of

reference:

i.

To review the role, structure and operations of DFls and commercial banks in

emerging operating environment and to suggest changes;

ii.

working capital finance by banks and DFls;

ili.

To examine whether DFls could be given increased access to short-term funds

and the regulatory framework needed for the purpose;

iv.

management practices and other related issues in DFls and commercial banks in

the wake of Capital Account Covertibility.

V.

Tomake such other recommendations as the Working Group may deem

appropriate.

Other members on the Working Group include:

ShriM.S.Verma,Chairman,StateBankofIndia;ShriK.V.Karmath,ChiefExecutive

Officer & Managing Director, Industrial Credit and Investment Corporation of India Ltd.,

Shri K.D. Agrawal, Chairman & Managing Director, Industrial Finance Corporation of

India Ltd., Shri M.G. Bhinde, Chairman & Managing Director, Bank of India and Shri A.T.

Pannir Selvam, Chairman & Managing Director, Union Bank of India.

Shri V. Subrahmanyam, Executive Director, Reserve Bank of India will be the Member-

within threemonths.

The demarcation of responsibilities between banks and DFls relating to working capital

transformation in the post financial sector reform era. This has led to a blurring of the

distinction between the lending operations of the two sets of institutions.On the

resources side, DFls' access to government guranteed bonds (SLR bonds), which used

access to short term funds subject to certain conditions. DFls have been seeking to raise

<!-- PAGE 2 -->

the ceiling on their access to short term funds and remove the restrictive conditions so as to bring them on par with banks. On the other hand, banks too have been seeking a level-playing field in areas such as, reserve requirements on DFIs in relation to their short term funds. Capital Account Convertibility when introduced is likely to bring about further changes in the operating environment necessitating advance action on the part of banks and DFIs to strengthen their organisations. The Working Group has been set up in the light of these changes and keeping in view the need for evolving an efficient and competitive financial system. (Alpana Killawala) **General Manager** **Press Release : 1997-98/472**
