To facilitate speedy clearing of the growing volume of paper instruments-the volume of cheques cleared at centers managed by the Reserve Bank stands at 440 million today, higher by 90 million compared to 1990-91 - the Reserve Bank had in 1987-89 introduced the Magnetic Ink Character Recognition (MICR) technology for cheque processing and clearing in four metropolitan cities. This has facilitated same day intra-city settlement of payment instruments. The Reserve Bank is now endeavouring to extend the MICR facility to additional 26 centers in accordance with the Saraf Committee recommendations. This will be in addition to separate clearing of high value cheques done by the Reserve Bank at four metros to give same day credit to large value cheques.

The Reserve Bank has also experimented with credit and debit clearing. The electronic clearing services (ECS) offered by the Reserve Bank at four metros receive credit instructions regarding payment of interest, dividend, etc., from the corporate houses in an electronic file and directly credit the recipient customer's account. Per contra, the customer's account can be debited directly for utility service payment such as, electricity or telephone bills through the ECS.

While intra city payments can be effected on the same day, inter city settlement still takes time. In order to expedite inter city clearing, the Reserve Bank has introduced electronic funds transfer system. At present the system is operational between Mumbai and Chennai and the efforts are on to connect the computerised clearing systems at the four metros for operationalising EFT among them. There are also other developments such as, the use of credit cards, automated teller machines (ATMs), shared payment network system being set up in Mumbai, etc., which will reduce the delays in payment mechanism. The Reserve Bank has also decided to set up a VSAT based communication network covering about 400 centers in the country for a closed user group of banks and financial institutions. This would not only facilitate real time settlement of financial transactions but help banks in their house keeping and MIS by allowing speedy transfer of data.

In order to minimise the risk involved in transactions in government securities, the Reserve Bank, in 1995 introduced a delivery versus payment (DvP) system in government securities transactions. The DvP system effects the debit and credit entries concurrently in securities and payment accounts of buyers and sellers thus reducing the risk involved in these transactions. The Reserve Bank has at present adopted the gross settlement system for settling the accounts. As the volumes in government securities grow, this will impose a restriction on banks and it might be necessary to give daytime overdraft facility to banks. The Reserve Bank is currently examining this issue.

Increasing use of technology, however, will have to be supported by appropriate changes in law and the changed attitudes of people using the technology. Financial stability is the prime concern of the central bank and an efficient and speedy payment and settlement system is central to financial stability. Learning from the experiences of other countries would thus be advantageous to the participants of the seminar, the Governor hoped.

In his introductory presentation, Mr. M. Andre Bascoul, Deputy Manager, BIS, said that in the past ten years, payment systems have indeed become a matter of prime responsibility for the central banks as they move over to a more modern arrangement or as sophistication of firms and consumers increases and a more developed financial sector emerges. According to Mr. Bascoul, even though institutional differences exist among central banks' involvement in systems and there are also many differences in the environment in which oversight of financial system takes place, while reforming payment systems and establishing oversight regimes, it is necessary to go through a process of intensive consultation and arrive at an eventual consensus.

Earlier, Shri R.V. Gupta, Deputy Governor, Reserve Bank of India welcomed the guests and the participants and stressed the need for training and change in the attitude of people for an effective use of technology.
