## Use GDP and IIP Data Series to track Economic Growth;

## Develop Institutional Means to bridge Data Gaps: RBI Group

A majority of conventional leading indicators used in developed economies are not available at monthly or quarterly intervals in the Indian context. Now that the Reserve Bank of India undertakes monetary policy review at quarterly intervals, inputs on movements of economic growth for quarterly reviews should also be of the same or higher frequency. Out of the currently available economic growth series, the quarterly GDP and monthly IIP series qualify as the reference series since the information regarding the turning points in economic growth cycle based on the leading indicators for these two reference series would be more robust as compared to dependence on a single series. This was suggested by a Technical Advisory Group set up by the Reserve Bank of India in its report.

The Reserve Bank had, in March 2006, set up the Technical Advisory Group on 'Development of Leading Economic Indicators for Indian Economy' in view of the felt need to monitor the movements of the Indian economy on a regular basis based on appropriate methodology and international best practices. The Group was required to suggest a feasible methodological framework for construction of co-incident, leading and lagging indicators and composite index for the Indian economy with a view to assisting monetary policy formulation and to guide and oversee its implementation. Chaired by Dr R B Barman, Executive Director, Reserve Bank of India, the Group had eminent economists, such as, Prof. Pami Dua, Prof. Ashima Goyal, Prof. Neeraj Hatekar, Prof. Amita Majumdar along with some senior RBI officials as members. The Group examined the existing literature, both domestic and international, on construction of Leading Economic Indicators. The empirical exercise was carried out using the techniques adopted by the international organisations and also based on the methodologies suggested in some research papers.

The Group has further recommended that the lead period in the case of quarterly data (i.e., non-agricultural GDP) should be at least two quarters ahead and in the case of monthly data (i.e., Index of Industrial Production), four months ahead. This is because in order to trigger timely appropriate counter cyclical measures, understanding the phases of expansions and contractions of economic activity is of paramount importance to the Reserve Bank of India. Also, the information on the turning points needs to be made available well in advance as the monetary policy measures impact after a certain lag.

The Group has noted that some variables, such as, housing starts, housing price index, capacity utilisation, etc., were not presently compiled at reasonable level of aggregation. Further, information on some important indicators, such as, employment, wages, sales, order books, inventory, savings and investment, etc., needed to be compiled at least at quarterly intervals so that they could be used to capture the turning points in the economy.

The Group has also underlined the importance of developing institutional capabilities for undertaking leading indicators work and has suggested that, during

## Background

The economic activity generally oscillates between the phases of expansion and contractions. Understanding the periodicity of expansionary and contractionary phases of economic activity has become the focus of macro- economic research, under the purview of business cycle. The study of business cycle enables the policy makers to trigger timely appropriate policy action in order to bring back the economic system to the desired path.

For a measurement of the business cycle, one has to observe a benchmark series, known as the reference series. The reference series encompasses the overall economic activity and could be adopted for policy purposes. Such a series may be a single series (indicator) or it may be a combination of certain economic series. To provide a more comprehensive measure of economic activity, the Composite Index of Coincidental Indicators (CICI) has been developed in many countries. The CICI are based on a basket of economic indicators, which represents the current economic activity.

The choice of economic indicators is a sensitive issue as the forecast performance depends on the quality of the indicators. The indicators should cover all the broad sectors in the economy, viz., real sector, financial sector, government sector, external sector, etc.

The construction of leading, coincident and lagging indicators is important from the viewpoint of forecasting business cycles, especially its turning points. Knowledge about the turning points is particularly important as most of forecasts go wrong at these points because the projections are generally based on linear models and the linear relationship characterised by a particular linear model breaks down around the turning points. From the viewpoint of the monetary policy, which involves transmission lags, correct forecasting of turning points is immensely important so as to facilitate appropriate policy actions to counter overheating or excessive downturns in the aggregate economic activity.
