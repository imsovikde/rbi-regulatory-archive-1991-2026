## Replenishment of Stock of Government Securities by the Reserve Bank for Open Market Operations

The Reserve Bank of India has been conducting open market operations through outright purchase/sale of Government securities and since June 2000, also through the Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF), which is intended as a window for adjusting day-to-day liquidity. Under the LAF, the Reserve Bank injects/withdraws liquidity through repos/reverse repos which are conducted with Government securities as collateral. While withdrawing liquidity from the system through reverse repos, the Reserve Bank has to provide Government securities as collateral from its stock in its investment account.

With periodic redemptions of Government securities, the stock of securities in the Reserve Bank's investment account gets depleted and, therefore, needs to be replenished, as often as necessary, through outright purchases in the secondary market (NDS-OM or auctions). This is so because in terms of the provisions of the Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management Act, 2003, with effect from April 2006, the Reserve Bank is precluded from purchasing Government securities in primary auctions.

Specific details of such replenishment operations undertaken on NDS-OM would be disclosed with one week's lag, in the Weekly Statistical Supplement.
