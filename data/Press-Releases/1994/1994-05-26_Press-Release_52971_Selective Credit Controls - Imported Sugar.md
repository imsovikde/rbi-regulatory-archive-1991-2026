## Selective Credit Controls -Imported Sugar

**(May 26, 19'J4)**

**At present released stocks of sugar attract a minimum margin of 75 per cent while against**

**warehouse receipts it is** 60 **per cent. Considering** **the price output situation, Government of India** has **placed imports of sugar under** OGL. With a **view to facilitating sizeable impons of sugar,** **Reserve Bank of India announced that with** **effect from May** 27, 1994 **the minimum margin** **on bank advances against stocks of sugar imported on a cash basis (sight Letter of Credit)**

**would be 25 per cent for a period not exceeding eight weeks after the arrival of sugar stocks in**

## India. This concession would be available up to

**end November,** 1994. **The concessional** **minimum margin on bank advances against** **imported sugar stocks would** be **applicable only** **at the first stage at the importer's level** and **this** **would not** be **available at the level of stockists** **and other traders in imported sugar. In this context banks should ensure that the irnponers**

## have not availed of suppliers' credit while importing the sugar. They may also evolve

## prudential norms of credit exposure while financing imported sugar.

**In the case of imports of sugar made on usance Letters of Credit (UC) viz. documents**

**against acceptance (D/ A) basis, banks should not, as far as possible, establish Letters of Credit**

**for more than 45 days on D/ A basis for iqiport of sugar. In case, however, banks establish**
