**Governor's Consultations on Monetary** **Management** **(October 12, 1994)** **Against the backdrop of recent monetary developments, the Governor, Reserve Bank of** **India, held consultations with a group of eminent economists on issues relating to monetary and** **exchange rate management in the overall context of economic i'eforms. The discussions centred on** **three major areas of concern, viz., the future stance of the monetary policy, the level and** **structure of interest rates and the impact of capital flows on the conduct of monetary policy.**

## The economists emphasised that price stability should remain the predominant concern

## of the monetary policy, even while stimulating

**real activity in the economy was important.** **Deliberating on the issue of interest rate deregulation, the economists cautioned against** **complete deregulation since it could entail systemic risks; it could also affect the** **profitability of the banking sector. It was pointed out that while investments were far more** **responsive to interest rates than aggregate savings, financial savings were influenced by** **interest rates. Therefore, in sequencing of economic reforms, interest rate deregulation** **might need to be undertaken gradually.** **While large accretions to foreign exchange reserves are a source of concern for monetary** **management, the economists recognised that sterilization through open market operations** **carry economic costs. Efforts to sterilize these inflows could raise interest rates and might** **make inflows self-perpetuisting. While taxing inflows could send a wrong signal to the** **investors at this stage, some mechanism would have to be evolved to moderate the cost of** **carrying the on-going build up of reserves and to contain the volatility associated with these** **flows. Suggestions were made for better supply management through more intensive utilization** **of foreign exchange inflows, as for example, by importation of critical items in short supply** **and building up infrastructure. Retirement of high cost external borrowings was also** **suggested as another measure. Some economists suggested a large degree of** **flexibility in exchange rate management to address the problem of capital inflows. The** **importance of factors other than price, such as, international environment and quality of** **products in stepping up exports was also emphasised.**

**It was felt that fiscal correction and strengthening of the pick up in real activity were** **cruci to the success of the refonn programme.**

Reducing the revenue component of the fiscal deficit and raising tax to GDP ratio by going ahead with generalised value added tax (VA 1) were suggested in this context.

In his concluding remarks, the Governor observed that there was a fair degree of consensus on a number of issues. He stated that though the stabilisation measures had been successful, there was a need for an enduring fiscal correction.

The Governor pointed out that supply side responses to structural reforms do take some time. He added that the recent performance of the manufacturing sector provided confidence that industrial growth in 1994-95 would be about seven per cent. There was also evidence ofa pick up in corporate investment. While noting the views expressed hy the economists on interest rates and exchange rates, the Governor said that proper timing and magnitude of policy changes were important. He also stated that apart from analytics it was necessary to recognise the problems at the ground level whenever policy changes were made.
