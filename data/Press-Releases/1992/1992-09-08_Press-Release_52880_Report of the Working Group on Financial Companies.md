The Group has recommended dismantling 'of the categories/classifications of NBFCs and application of uniform regulations for all. At present NBFCs are broadly classified into loan, investment, equipment leasing, hire purcha se, m ut ual benefit financing companies, residuary non-banking companies and housing finance companies. According to the Group, all the existing deposit taking companies with net owned funds of Rs. 50 lakhs and over should compulsorily register with the regulatory authority. The existing companies with net owned funds below this cut off point may, if they so prefer, opt for registration. The existing companies with net owned funds below Rs.50 lakhs and not registered with the regulatory authorities may be allowed to accept public deposits at a lower level than that stipulated for the registered companies. The Group also suggests that the cut off point for deposit acceptance may be periodically reviewed ..

The Group has favoured that the focus of NBFC regulation will be shifted from liabilities-side to assets-side of the NBFC balance sheet. With this in view the Group has prescribed capital adequacy standards based on risk-weighted assets as prescribed for commercial banks. Commercial banks are expected to achieve capital adequacy ratio at the rate of 8 per cent of the risk weighted assets by March 1995. The Group has recommended that the capital adequacy norms be worked out by the regulatory authority in coordination with the representatives of NBFCs, self regulating organisations and the Institute of Chartered Accountants. The exercise of computing the risk weighted assets and credit conversion' measures may be completed by end March 1994 and the capital adequacy ratio be introduced from April of 1995.

Till the capital adequacY framework is introduced. the existing limit on deposit acceptance may be continued subject to an overall debt-equity ratio of 15:1. The Group has also recommended compulsory credit
