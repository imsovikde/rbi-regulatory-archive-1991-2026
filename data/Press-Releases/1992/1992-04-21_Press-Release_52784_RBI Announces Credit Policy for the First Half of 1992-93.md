# RBI Announces Credit Policy for the First Half of 1992-93

# (April 21, 1992)

Announcing the credit policy for the first half of 1992-93, Shri S. Venkitaramanan, Governor, Reserve Bank of India, said that the monetary policy in 1991-92 was governed primarily by the need to controlinflation and the concern about the foreign exchange reserves position. The objectives of monetary policy for 1992-93 have to take into account the need to revive industrial and agricultural production and encourage exports keeping in mind the primacy of the objectives of control of inflation and the balance of payments stability. Monetary policy has to take note of Government's declared objective of reduction of the gross fiscal deficit from 6.5 per cent of GDP in 1991-92 to 5 per cent of GDP in 1992-·

93.Government has also taken positive steps to bring down the rate of inflation; the objective of policy would be to bring it down to 8 per cent in 1992-93. To realise the full anti-inflationary impact of the reduction in the budget deficit it is necessary to bring about a sharp reduction in
the pace of monetary expansion (M3) from

18.6 per cent in 1991-92 to less than 11 per cent in 1992-93. Growth of money supply has a number of contributing factors. the most powerful being the increase of credit by RBI and the banks to Government. It is proposed to reorganise the gilt-edged market in such a way that Government will decrease its dependence on credit from RBI and the banks. This will be an important element of monetary and credit policy for 1992-93. At the same time, once credit to Government preempts less resources of the banking sector, it should be possible to provide for a broadening of credit availability to agriculture. trade and industry to support the revival of activity in the economy. Addressing the Chief Executives of major scheduled commercial banks in Bombay on April 21, 1992,· the Governor has advised scheduled commercial banks to plan their credit budgets on the basis of a working estimate of the increase in aggregate deposits of Rs. 27,000 crore (l 1.7 per cent) during 1992-93. The increase in aggregate deposits during the first half of 1992-93 is estimated at around Rs. 14,000 crore. He stressed that the lendable resources of banks should be used tq support the revival of activity in both the agricultural and industrial sectors. Particular attention of banks is invited to genuine· credit requirements of small scale industries and exports which should be fully met. The changes in measures include a discontinuation of the IO per cent incremental cash reserve ratio together with a reduction in the interest on banks' cash balances with the Reserve Bank, withdrawal of the incremental non-food credit-deposit ratio, rationalisation of lending rates, deposit rates and export refina_nce rates, relaxation of restrictions on credit for consumer ,durables, tightening of selective credit controls, increase in the limits of Certificates of Deposit, liberalisation of terms for issue of Commercial Paper, introduction of -a new scheme of Money

## (I) Introduction of Longer Maturity

## Treasury Bills

The development of a Government : securities market is an important step towards evolution of tools of monetary control other than. those provided by reserve requirements and credit controls. It is also the intention of policy to ensure that Government's credit needs 'are met more and more directly from the market instead of through preemption of deposit resources. Keeping this in view, certain measures of widening of the Treasury Bill market are being undertaken. With a view

to developing the Treasury Bill as a monetary instrument with flexible rates and to provide a financial instrument with an intermediate maturity between dated securities and the 91 Days Treasury Bills, a 182 Days Treasury Bill was introduced in November 1986. The 182 Days Treasury Bill with liquidity provided by the Discount and Finance House of India has become an attractive money market instrument and the outstandings of these Treasury Bills reached a peak. of Rs. 4,090 crore in January 1992. As part of the overall development of the Government securities market, the Government of India propose to float Treasury Bills of varying maturities up to 364 days on an auction basis. The auction scheme for these new instruments will be identical to that for the 182 Days Treasury Bill. Thus, at the next auction on April 28, 1992, Treasury Bills of 364 Days will be auctioned. Henceforth, the Reserve Bank will announce the maturity ofthe Treasury Billfor.. each auction. The introduction of Treasury Bills of varying maturities would offer investors a wider choice for investing in different instruments and thereby foster the development of the Government securities. market.
