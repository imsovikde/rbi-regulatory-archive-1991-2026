# Report of the Committee on Currency Management

# (August 20, 1992)

**The phenomenal rates of growth in currency in the last two decades and in** **particular, since 1975 would indicate that unless immediate action is initiated to evolve a** **long term strategy, overhaul the existing machinery for the efficient management of** **currency and rationalise and streamline the present systems and procedures, the Reserve** **Bank of India's ability to discharge its primary** **central banking function** would **be** **increasingly in jeopardy. Against this back ground, a high powered Committee on** **Currency Management was constituted by the** **then Governor in December** 1988 **under the** **chairmanship of Shri** P. R. **Nayak, Deputy** **Governor, to go into the whole gamut of issues in · currency management. The** **Committee submitted its report on 30th** **September 1989.**

## (a) Findings of the Committee

**The Committee looked into the current problems in the area of currency management** **and focussed its attention on the currency scenario by the turn of the century and the** **first decade of the next century. It found that the systems and procedures which were** **adequate to deal with the circulation of a little over l00 million pieces in 1935 were unable to** **bear the pressure of the present volume of notes in circulation which was of the order of** **20,500 miUion pieces in March *1991.* Going by the rate of growth in currency over the last ten** **years, the volume of notes in circulation by** **2,000 AD is expected to expand to about** **61,000 million pieces with a value of a bout** **Rs. 1,69,000 crores. It also observed that while the notes in circulation more than doubled** **between 1980 and 1989, fresh notes supplied by the Government Presses rose only by 20** **per c·ent making it necessary to salvage even poor quality notes and re-issue them to the** **pubHc, leading, in turn, to a *rising* nood of unserviceable notes offered for withdrawal**

(b) **Strategy** According to the Committee, the strategy should seek, first and foremost, to contain the number of notes in circulation during the next decade at a level not very much higher than at present and which is commensurate with the capacity of note presses and an acceptable quality. of notes secondly, to rationalise the distribution and collection of currency with a view to minimising avoidable cross movements of currency notes across the country and to reduce the return flow to the Reserve Bank of India of notes which are still good enough to circulate; thirdly, to stream line the handling and processing of fresh and soiled notes by eliminating unproductive and repetitive procedures and fourthly, to provide a work atmosphere for the employees which seeks to motivate them in doing their work.
(c) **Recommendations** The main recommendations of the Committee are as under:
(i) The single most important measure to
control growth of currency in the next decade is the coinisation of Re. l, Rs. 2 and Rs. 5 notes which constitute about 57% in terms of volume but only 7% in terms of value.

(ii) Establishment of Currency Transit Centres at suitable locations, which will receive, store, re-distribute fresh notes received from presses to currency chests and to receive soiled notes despatched from the

chests and re-transmit them to Issue Department for examination and eventual destruction.

(iii) Introduction of new systems of packaging of notes in shrink packed and heatseald transparent polythene sheets aimed at reducing the time-consuming and repetitive stages of handling of notes by various agencies in the journey of notes from the presses to the customers.

(iv) Introduction of a new procedure to simultaneous examination, verification and destruction of notes in issue Offices toobviate present formalities of handling over/ taking over the balances by various functionaries of the office.

(v) Introduction of note counting
machines in Issue Offices and chests, coin dispensing machines at counters of Issue Offices to provide efficient customer service, electro-mechanical systems for movement of remittance boxes in security areas and high speed/ capacity modern shredders for destruction of notes in Issue Offices.

(vi) Improvement inthe work environment in Issue Offices in general and Cash Department in particular, with a view to motivating and instilling a sense of belonging amongst the well-qualified staff who perform repetitive/ monotonous ·work.
