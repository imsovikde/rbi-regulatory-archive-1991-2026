# <u>Development Research Group Study Series No.17 :</u>

# "The Impact of the Uruguay Round on Growth and Structure of India Economy"

## January 6, 1999

The Development Research Group (DRG) of the Reserve Bank of India has brought out its Study Series No. 17 entitled "The Impact of the Uruguay Round on Growth and Structure of Indian Economy". The study is authored jointly by Dr.R. Bharadwaj, Professor and Chairman, Institute for Educational Research and Development, Bangalore along with Shri Sujan Hajra, Research Officer of the Department of Economic Analysis and Policy and Smt.Nishita Raje, Assistant Adviser, Monetary Policy Department of the Reserve Bank of India. The DRG undertakes quick and effective policy-oriented research backed by strong analytical and empirical basis on subjects of current interest. DRG studies are the outcome of collaborative efforts between experts from outside the Reserve Bank and the pool of research talents within the Bank. These studies are released for wider circulation with a view to generating constructive discussion among professional economists and policy makers. <u>The views expressed in these studies are those of the</u> <u>authors and do not reflect the views of the Reserve Bank.</u>

The present study analyses the impact of the Uruguay Round on the Indian economy. The Uruguay Round is the longest Round of the General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade (GATT) which began in September 1986 at Punta del Este, Uruguay and concluded in Geneva, Switzerland on December 15, 1993. The negotiation of the Uruguay Round, was formally concluded on April 15, 1994 when 116 member nations signed the agreement in Marrakkesh, Morocco. The Uruguay Round has far reaching implications for the world economy in view of the establishment of the WTO in January 1, 1995, enhancing the scope for multilateral mechanisms for supervision as well as freer international trade order and also due to the inclusion of three new fields within the fold of multilateral trade negotiations viz, trade in services, trade-related investment measures (TRIMS) and trade related intellectual property rights (TRIPS).

In this context, the study attempts to provide an estimate of likely consequences of the Uruguay Round for the Indian economy under alternative policy situations. The study has used a general equilibrium approach to study various impacts of the Uruguay Round on India's levels of output, foreign trade, prices and employment. It has focussed on 15 major sectors which are the thrust sectors for the Uruquay Round negotiations. The sectors are paddy, wheat, pulses, cotton, sugar, beverages, tobacco and tobacco products, six textile sub-sectors, wood & paper products, drugs & medicines and the residual sector.

The results of the study indicate that the overall impact of the Uruguay Round agreements is likely to be beneficial for the Indian economy in terms of growth, foreign trade and employment. Assuming that world prices change by the amounts indicated by GATT and OECD, GNP at constant prices may increase by 1.4 per cent per annum,

At the disaggregated level, sectors within agriculture and agro-based industries are likely to benefit from the outcome of the Uruguay Round. These results also indicate the need for carrying out restructuring in certain segments of the textile sector. These industries require concerted efforts to enhance their efficiency so that they can face external competition under the emerging freer international trade order.

From the perspective of a policy framework the study points out that further trade orientation of the economy, a proper positioning of the exchange rate and a stable and consistent commercial policy for certain sectors will be needed to maximise gains from the Uruguay Round for the Indian economy.
