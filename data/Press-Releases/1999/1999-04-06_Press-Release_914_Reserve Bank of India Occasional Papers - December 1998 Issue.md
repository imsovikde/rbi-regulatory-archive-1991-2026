# Reserve Bank of India Occasional Papers-December 1998 Issue

The December1998 issue of the Reserve Bank of India Occasional Papers (OPs) contains three research articles, two notes and three book reviews. <u>They reflect the research efforts of the</u> <u>authors and their personal views.</u>

The first article of this volume 'Recent Divergence between wholesale and Consumer Prices in India-A Statistical Exploration' by G.P. Samanta and Sharmishtha Mitra explores the short-run and long-run relationship between the Wholesale Price Index (WPI) and the Consumer Price Index for Industrial Workers (CPIIW). The study examines the divergence between WPI and CPIIW and notes that the two series diverged noticeably since May 1995. While a part of this divergence is explained by the differences in commodity basket and weighting pattern inherent in two price indices, there are also other factors which are at work in giving rise to this outcome. Empirical results of the paper indicate that while removal of sources of divergence due to commodity basket and weighting pattern improves the short-run relationship between CPIIW and WPI, the improvement is not so impressive for the long-term relationship. This points to other factors such as price quotations, differences in market behaviours, etc., which may have a role in the recent divergence in the relationship between CPIIW and WPI.

The second article of this volume 'Integration of Financial Markets in India : An Empirical Evaluation' by B.K. Bhoi and S.C. Dhal empirically evaluates the degree of integration of India's financial markets in the post-liberalisation period. The paper points out that although a fully competitive environment is yet to emerge in India, several segments of the financial market have achieved operational efficiency. It shows that the 91-day Treasury Bill rate has the potential to emerge as a reference rate in the Indian financial market. The paper also finds that India's financial markets are getting increasingly integrated at the short-end of the market, such as, money market, credit market, Government securities market since April 1993. However, capital market is least integrated with the rest of the financial sector. There are early indications about the integration of money and forex market in India. However integration of domestic and overseas financial markets is not robust. The movement of various interest rates in uniform directions, nevertheless shows an encouraging sign of the growing maturity of the financial markets and their sensitivity to monetary policy. This has significant implications for the transmission of monetary policy, in so far as the quantum channel is becoming increasingly obsolete in view of the rising importance of interest rate and asset price channels.

The third article of the volume "Liquidity Effects on the Term Structure of Government Securities Market in India" by Himanshu Joshi attempts to undertake an empirical assessment of the term structure of interest rates in the Indian economy in order to identify the possible regularities of relationship among various interest rates in government securities market employing the cointegration and common trends analysis. The study has chosen the following term structure of interest rates (i) very short term inter-bank call money rate, (ii) cut off yield on short term 91 day Treasury Bills, (iii) cut off yield on medium term 364 day Treasury Bills, and, (iv) redemption yield on long term GOI dated securities. The results of the study suggests that the absence of a unique common trend among interest rates implies that the long run movement of any one interest rate in not dominated by the movements of other interest rates. The presence

of cointegration, however, suggests a long run interlocking of interest rates across markets and a possibility of their common response to changes in expectations about future monetary policy and/or economic fundamentals. Finally, the results also suggest that structural policies pursued by the Central Bank could be of crucial importance in facilitating market integration.

The present issue also contains two notes. The note titled "The Economics of Amartya Sen-A Review" by Sunando Roy attempts to provide an overview of Prof.Amartya Sen's contributions to economics. The review looks at the major contributions of Prof.Amartya Sen in welfare economics and social choice theory, economic inequality and poverty, famines and hunger, concepts of development and economic reforms and liberalisation. The note analyses Amartya Sen's work in welfare economics in the context of the growth of modern public choice theory. It highlights Prof, Sen's penetrating analysis of the economics of inequality in its various dimensions and his concerns for gender inequality. The note has placed many significant contributions of Prof.Sen in the areas of poverty, famine and social inequality in a proper perspective. the note attempts to identify the forces that unite the diverse world of Prof. Amartya Sen, including his concerns for egalitarianism, the centrality of human freedom and a strong belief in the democratic state.

The note on "Managing Financial Risk in India-A Note" by Shyamala Gopinath and A. Prasad examines the importance of risk management exercise for the participants in Indian financial market. The note stresses the need for addressing certain policy and institutional issues in developing a market for risk-taking, risk sharing and risk-diversification in India. The suggestions provided by the note include, among others, development of the repo market, encouraging Revolving Underwriting Facility (RUF), introduction of asset-based derivatives such as strips and Asset Backed Securities, introduction of credit-default swaps, developing the market for forward rate agreement and allowing interest rate swaps. The note argues that active use of derivatives will require the existence of a term money market for six month to one-year tenure as a precondition.

The study observes that a major reason for the lack of term money market is the absence of the practice of ALM system among banks. The recent RBI guidelines on ALM are expected to contribute to the evolvement of an ALM system which would help banks lend on a term basis and also offer two-way quotes in the market. This would act as a catalyst for development of a term money market. Some of the other suggestions emerging from the study for developing a term money market are improving the flexibility of banks to fix PLR across the term structure of loan and extending loans on a fixed rate basis. The note adds that given the current structure of the Indian Rupee money market and its characteristics, introduction of derivatives should be undertaken in a gradual manner. A suggested sequencing pattern could be to develop market for forward rate agreements and interest rate swaps first before moving on to options.
