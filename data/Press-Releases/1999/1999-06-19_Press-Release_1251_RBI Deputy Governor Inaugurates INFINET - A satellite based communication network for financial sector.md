# RBI Deputy Governor Inaugurates INFINET-A satellite based communication network for financial sector

## June 19, 1999

The Reserve Bank of India has given high priority to bringing about efficient payment systems. The design, management, regulation and supervision of electronic payment systems would soon become a major focal point of policy deliberations in India. The Reserve Bank is, therefore, moving inexorably towards the goal of providing an effective, efficient and speedy payment and settlement system. The INFINET would give the much-required fillip in enabling information flow across branches of banks and would, thus, be the backbone for transmission of messages, information, data and funds for the entire banking sector in the country.

This was stated by Shri S P Talwar, Deputy Governor, Reserve Bank of India. He was inaugurating the INFINET network at the Institute for Research and Development in Banking Technology (IDRBT) at Hyderabad today.

IDRBT was set up by Reserve Bank of India at Hyderabad in 1996. The institute is fully funded by the Reserve Bank of India as an autonomous centre for development and research in banking technology.

The Indian Financial Network (INFINET) is a satellite based communication network for a closed user group of banking and financial sector. Set up by the Reserve Bank of India in the form of a satellite based Very Small Aperture Terminal (VSAT) network to start with, this network operates in the extended C-Band and will have about 450 VSATs. INFINET is going to be the payment systems backbone in the county which will cater mainly to inter-bank applications, such as, Real Time Gross Settlement (RTGS) System, Delivery Vs Payment (DVP), government transactions, automatic clearing house (ACH), etc. It will, in course of time, be a blend of VSAT technology and high-speed terrestrial network. Plans are afoot to connect about 18 major business centers through high-speed terrestrial links. Both, VSAT and the terrestrial network of the INFINET will co-exist by drawing from the strengths of each other. Their deployment will depend on suitability, availability and accessibility.

Explaining the background for going into VSAT based technology for the country's payment system, the Deputy Governor stated that as the economic activity grew, it would need speedier funds flow and safer channels for its transmission. Satellite technology provided for speedier and more secure communication modes than the traditional terrestrial modes. Shri Talwar mentioned that the INFINET stood for the Reserve Bank's commitment to utilise the best of leading edge technology to enable the banking sector to achieve international standards. He hoped that with the operationalisation of INFINET, one of the biggest bottlenecks of the present times, namely the lack of connectivity of banks, would be eased to a great extent.

Focussing on the current scenario in the banking industry, the Deputy Governor stated that the need to provide first class service to customers of banks and other financial entities at affordable costs was the driving force behind the current heightened activity in the banking industry. Customers, on their part, had also become sophisticated and had very convincingly articulated their needs. They appreciate prompt and value-added service. Banks, therefore, had devoted considerable attention to the processes that help their products to be acceptable. Technology provided the key to such processes.

The Deputy Governor gave the broad details of the INFINET project and stated that in the first phase, VSAT have been installed in all the sixteen offices of the Banking Department of the Reserve Bank, at the Ministry of Finance in New Delhi and at selected sites of the public sector banks. Mentioning that for the present, the use of the network would be limited by the constraint of the transponder space allocation, the Deputy Governor added that the one-eighth of transponder space allocated on extended C-band on INSAT 2B would need to be expanded as user institutions place more applications on the network and as more institutions join the INFINET User Group. The demand for communication bandwidth would increase as the users find the immense utility of the network. It would, therefore, be necessary to eventually get a whole transponder to be allocated for purposes of networking, he argued.

The Deputy Governor hoped that when fully operationalised, the INFINET would help optimise the funds flow management by banks, aid decision making, enable faster reporting of government account transactions, flow of government funds and improve customer service all around. He pointed out that provision of a country-wide network is only a beginning. For effective and optimal utilisation of the INFINET, computerisation of bank branches as also, standardisation of hardware, operating systems, platforms and application softwares throughout the banking industry would be imperative. The INFINET would give impetus to bank computerisation to cover bulk of banking business and to interface with the banking system. This in turn would help in better housekeeping. While interconnectivity would ensure optimal utilisation of funds, VSAT technology would help not only in speedier funds transfer but also help speedier information flows. which would result in better supervision, monitoring at the level of controlling offices, and decision making at the corporate level.

According to the Deputy Governor, all major bank branches in commercial important cities of India should be brought under total computerisation as expeditiously as possible. The next step would be to link all computerised branches to their service branches, treasury/ funds departments and controlling offices. Connectivity to treasury/ funds department would ensure optimal utilisation of funds and connectivity to controlling offices would aid in empirical decision making. All banks should, therefore, aim at putting in place such intra-bank hierarchical networks. Once intra-bank connectivity was complete, and with inter-bank connectivity being provided by the INFINET, more than 70 per cent of banking business would have the advantage of access to an on-line real time environment.

The Deputy Governor also envisaged the usage of the INFINET backbone for information exchange between banks. Credit information on borrowers’ accounts, for instance, could be shared among banks to their mutual advantage, he pointed out. Information that was essential for management to take decisions, the Reserve bank guides, circulars, etc., could all be transmitted through the INFINET. Inter-bank communication which some times tends to take a long duration could get completed very quickly over the INFINET. Transmission of data for warehousing purposes and for the requirement of the Reserve Bank for offsite surveillance, information to controlling offices from branches, were some of the areas in which the INFINET could be effectively used. He hoped that all these developments, taking place with the aid of technology, would provide satisfaction to the ultimate users of banking services.

In his key-note address, Dr. A. Vasudevan, Executive Director, Reserve bank of India pointed out that the INFINET clearly gets the distinction of being a forerunner of an efficient telecommunications backbone for the banking and financial sector. Inter-city Electronic Clearing Service (ECS) under which corporate customers at one place could effect credits to accounts of customers situated anywhere within the reach of the ECS could be one of the applications of INFINET, the Executive Director stated. This, he said, would pave the way ultimately for paperless funds transfers and would greatly minimise the levels of clearing related reconciliation entries at banks. This could be extended to Electronic Funds Transfer, Centralised Funds Management System (CFMS) comprising the Centralised Funds Enquiry System (CFES) for banks maintaining current accounts with the Reserve Bank and ultimately to the establishment of a Real Time Gross Settlement System for the country. INFINET, Dr. Vasudevan pointed out, would be an ideal medium for transmission of both messages and funds for the RTGS system.

Dr Vasudevan mentioned that work for finalisation of the message formats for inter- banks applications was under progress. Once message formats were standardised for use across banks, message transfers for a wide variety of applications that take place between banks would take place effectively, efficiently and quickly. This may also open up facilities for Electronic Data Interchange (EDI) between banks and their constituents and perhaps pave the way for the introduction of E-Commerce in the country.

Earlier, Dr. V P Gulati, Director, IDRBT welcomed the guests and gave a brief introduction of the role of IDRBT in furthering research in banking technology and the INFINET project. He stated that the IDRBT's mission was to be a think-tank for the promotion of technology solution to improve the functioning of the banking and financial sector. He stated that the Institute would be the instrument for development and adoption of technologies in the banking sector. Its major focus would be on research, development and education activities.

Dr. Gulati informed that the institute had already undertaken a number of research projects in the area of computer based training, certification management by Certification Authority, data mining for the analysis of credit card transactions. The Institute was actively involved with Indian Institute of Technology, Mumbai on a collaborative project
