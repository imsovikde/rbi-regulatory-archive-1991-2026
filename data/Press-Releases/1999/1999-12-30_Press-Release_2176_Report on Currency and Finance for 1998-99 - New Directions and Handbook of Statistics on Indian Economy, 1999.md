# Report on Currency and Finance for 1998-99 - New Directions and Handbook of Statistics on Indian Economy, 1999

<!-- PAGE 1 -->

## Report on Currency and Finance for 1998-99 - New Directions and

## Handbook of Statistics on Indian Economy, 1999

## December 30, 1999

## Background

The Reserve Bank of India today released its yearly Report on Currency and Finance for 1998-99. This Report is being published since the inception of the Reserve Bank of India, the first of which pertained to the years 1935-36 and 1936-37 and published in 1937. A non- statutory Report, the objective of the Report on Currency and Finance has been to provide information and analysis. The document has been a source of standard reference material for policy-makers, researchers and keen observers of the Indian economy. The Reserve Bank also brings out two statutory publications each year, namely its Annual Report and the Report on Trend and Progress of Banking in India. Both these reports precede the Report on Currency and Finance. As a result of enhancements brought about in these two reports in the recent years, the Report on Currency and Finance is sometimes viewed as more or less a duplication of the Annual Report and the Report on Trend and Progress of Banking in India. Recently there has also been expression of interest in a publication by the Reserve Bank that focusses on a theme in addition to providing information on time series basis on different aspects of the economy on a regular basis.

## New Directions : Thematic and Research Oriented

Against this background, the Reserve Bank has, this year made the Report on Currency and Finance thematic and research oriented. **Essentially a research document, the Report has** **been prepared by economists of the Reserve Bank who have worked on different areas** **of the economy with focus on the theme of *structural transformation of the Indian economy* since the eighties.**

## Contents

The Report has eleven chapters. They cover macro economy and the real, monetary, fiscal, financial markets and external sectors, as in the earlier Reports. One of the chapters focusses on reforms of the international monetary and financial system and discusses issues such as, strengthening multilateral surveillance, choice of exchange rate regime, capital controls and capital account liberalisation, transparency and information disclosure and the role of private sector in crisis resolution.

**Handbook on Statistics**

The Report is released along with the *Handbook of Statistics on Indian Economy*. The *Handbook* is being issued in place of the Volume II of the Report on Currency and Finance. The *Handbook* has a wider coverage and provides data on time series on major macroeconomic indicators for a longer period (as compared with data series for a shorter period in the erstwhile Volume II of the Report on Currency and Finance). The volume contains 198 tables. The first volume released, in 1998 contained 135 tables. A new section on major growth rates and ratios of important economic indicators has also been added. The data are drawn from within the Reserve Bank and from outside published sources.
