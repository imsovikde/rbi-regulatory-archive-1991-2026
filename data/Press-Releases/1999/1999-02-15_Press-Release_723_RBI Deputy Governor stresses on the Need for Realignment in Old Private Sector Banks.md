# RBI Deputy Governor stresses on the Need for Realignment in Old Private Sector Banks

## February 15, 1999

The old private sector banks, which are 26 in number, need to realign themselves in order to bring in synergy and economies of scale to their operations. This was stated by Shri S.P.Talwar, Deputy Governor, Reserve Bank of India at a conference of the Chairmen of private sector banks today. The conference was held at the National Institute for Bank Management, Pune. Elaborating this, the Deputy Governor stated that the private banks should go, if necessary, for voluntary mergers among themselves. He clarified that the mergers need not necessarily be between a weak and strong bank but can also be between two strong banks as proven by recent global experiments.

Highlighting capital adequacy as the most important issue for the private sector banks, the Deputy Governor stated that reaching the prescribed level of capital adequacy and enhancing the networth were crucial for these banks. He explained that additional capitalisation was essential especially for the old private banks for their business growth and match the capital prescriptions for dealing with various emerging risk weighted products in Indian banking system. He, therefore, urged these banks to make efforts to not only reach the prescribed capital level standards but attempt to even keep the future requirement while augmenting their capital base.

While discussing the issue of capital adequacy, the Deputy Governor alluded to the estimates of Rs. 13,500 crore being mentioned in some quarters that may be required as additional capital funds by the public sector banks. Stating that these estimates were far off the mark, the Deputy Governor explained that the public sector banks despite their largeness would need to mobilise externally only around Rs 5,400 crore, that too only 13 nationalised banks and 5 associate banks of the State Bank of India as additional capital funds to meet the proposed Capital and Risk Assets Ratio (CRAR) requirement of 10 per cent by year 2002. This is despite assuming an average annual growth at 15 per cent in their risk weighted assets, conservative estimate on return on assets at 0.8 per cent and 12 per cent increase by way of retained earnings based on past trends over this period. Again, a large proportion (Rs. 3,350 crore) of these capital funds are required by the strong banks and easily mobilisable from the capital market either through equity or subordinated debt. Further, the needs of associated banks would easily be met by a cash rich SBI.

The Deputy Governor lamented the fact that the old private sector banks were slow in modernisation efforts. He pointed out that with globalising and because of the ongoing revolution in information technology (IT) and electronic funds transfer and payment system areas, financial systems have undergone fundamental changes. There was, thus, an immediate need for introduction of information and control systems, particularly in many of the old private sector banks to help them make strategic decisions. He also stressed on the need for urgently putting in place systems for risks and asset liability management in these banks.

The Deputy Governor exhorted the private sector banks to strive towards achieving Y2K compliance by the prescribed deadline. He pointed out that banks may be subject to penal

measures in case of persisting non-compliance beyond the prescribed deadline of March 1999. Institutions have also been advised to put in place appropriate contingency plans to take care of possible system failures.

Later, the Deputy Governor and Shri M.S. Verma, Honorary Advisor to the Reserve Bank had an interactive session with the participants. Shri Verma advised the participants to pay special attention to structuring their credit portfolios. He explained that while the Reserve Bank has, as a regulatory authority, prescribed the desirable prudential norms, banks in their own interest should also have their own internal lending and prudential norms. Lending, he said, should be a part of regular portfolio building and should happen as a part of strategy and policy which are clearly defined.

## Alpana Killawala General Manager
