# Submission of Statutory Return on Liquid Assets Compulsory for all NBFCs

## April 5, 1999

The Reserve Bank of India has advised that all non-banking financial companies (NBFCs) including residuary non-banking companies (NBFCs) irrespective of the size of their net owned fund or whether public deposits are held by them or not, are required to submit to the Reserve Bank the statutory quarterly return on liquid assets. It has been clarified that all NBFCs irrespective of the size of their NOF and including those whose applications for Certificate of Registration have been rejected, are required to furnish this quarterly return as per prescribed forms. The NBFCs have been advised to submit the return to the respective Regional Offices of the Department of Non-Banking Financial Companies of the Reserve Bank having jurisdiction over the company and not to the Central Office.

The NBFCs have been further advised that the companies not holding public deposits should not only submit 'nil' information in respect of public deposits but they must also fill up the information about net owned funds (NoF) as per the last audited balance sheet in the space provided for in the format of the return. The NBFCs have been cautioned that any default or persistent non-compliance with this requirement will attract penal provisions under the Reserve Bank of India Act including cancellation of Certificate of Registration, if already issued or rejection of the application for such a registration, if still pending with the Reserve Bank.

## Background

In terms of the statutory provisions of the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934, non-banking financial companies are required to maintain liquid assets as a specific percentage of public deposits held on the last working day of the second preceding quarter. The Reserve Bank has prescribed that the liquid assets requirement will be 15 per cent of such deposits from April 1,

1999. The companies are also required to furnish quarterly return on liquid assets to the Reserve Bank in compliance of the statutory requirement. Although it was earlier clarified that the NBFCs not holding public deposits need not submit such return to the Reserve Bank, in view of the large scale non-compliance of this requirement by NBFCs, particularly those having NOF below Rs.25 lakh and still holding public deposits, the matter has been reviewed. With the Reserve Bank shifting its regulatory focus to the NBFCs holding public deposits, ascertaining at any point of time, the number and names of NBFCs which accept/hold public deposits is considered to be necessary in order to direct the supervisory attention to such companies and also to protect the interest of their depositors. Keeping this in view, the Reserve Bank has decided that all NBFCs irrespective of the size or the nature of their operations must submit this statutory return. It may be recalled that the Reserve Bank had called for such return as on December 31, 1998 in the month of February. The information/data gathered through this return are being presently analysed and necessary action is being taken.
