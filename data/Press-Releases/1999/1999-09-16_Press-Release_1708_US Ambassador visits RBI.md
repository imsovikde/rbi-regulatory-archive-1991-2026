# US Ambassador visits RBI

## September 16, 1999

Mr. Richard Celeste, US Ambassador to India met the heads of the financial institutions including IDBI, LIC, GIC and UTI and banks in the Reserve Bank of India to discuss financial sector reforms in India, universal banking and issues relating to insurance business in the wake of the proposed Insurance Regulatory Authority Bill. He was accompanied by Mr. David Good, Consul General, and Mr. Jerrold Mallory, Consul (Political/Economics), Mr. Norman Sorensen, Principal International, Shri Dalip Verma, American International Group, Shri Sanjay Sachedev, Principal Financial Group, Shri Krishna Monie, Sr. Economic Adviser and Shri Zubin Kapadia, Vice Consul (Economic Affairs). The Reserve Bank was represented by the Deputy Governors, Executive Directors and other senior officials.

The main focus of the discussion was the potential for business in insurance sector, introduction of insurance products and technological back-up for providing insurance services both in life and general insurance. The participants felt that keeping in view the need for long term funds for development of infrastructure sector as well as other industrial sectors the broadening of the insurance sector was an important issue. It was indicated the Insurance Regulatory Authority Bill was yet to be passed by the Parliament and both, the Insurance Regulatory Authority and the Reserve Bank were examining the role of banks in undertaking insurance business in consultation with the Ministry of Finance.

The issues relating to the universal banking were also discussed and it was pointed out by the Reserve Bank that there is no legal restriction for the banks and development financial institutions to convert themselves into universal banking entities. It was only a matter of time when both could evolve as universal banks keeping in view the need to maintain parity in applicability of the regulatory requirements. A brief overview was also given about the banking sector reforms and the stable progress that India had made in this direction.
