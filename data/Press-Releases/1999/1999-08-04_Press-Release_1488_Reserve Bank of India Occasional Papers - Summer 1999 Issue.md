# Reserve Bank of India Occasional Papers-Summer 1999 Issue

## August 4, 1999

The Summer 1999 issue of the Reserve Bank of India Occasional Papers (OPs) contains three articles, one review article and two book reviews. They reflect the research efforts of the authors and their personal views.

In recent years, structural changes in financial systems and the integration of financial markets across borders has brought about fundamental changes in the concept of money. Ever since societies have invested central banks with the mandate of managing money, central banks have found it necessary to enunciate what money is, or more specifically, what money does. In view of the growing portfolio diversification of the public to include foreign currency denominated assets/liabilities and evolution of new financial instruments which redefine the concept of moneyness, the article on 'Residency in Money supply' by Deba Prasad Rath and Indranil Sen Gupta examines the implications of the recommendations of the recent Working Group on Money Supply for an economy in the process of opening up. The article empirically demonstrates that a measure of money, defined on a residency basis, performs better than the conventional aggregates in explaining inflation. The new money aggregates are recommended as efficient guideposts for the future conduct of monetary policy.

The principal consideration in the public's assessment of the mandate placed with the central bank is whether or not the central bank has been able to provide to it a money that it can trust; money which does not lose value in relation to transactions over time. The credibility of the central bank has come to be regarded as synonymous with its inflation record; however, even in countries where inflation mandates have been legislated, it has been recognised that there are several factors which impact upon inflation but remain outside the purview of the central bank's control. The second article "Core Inflation in India : Measurement and Policy Perspectives" by G.P. Samanta argues that the accountability of the central bank with regard to its inflation performance needs to be provided for in terms of those elements of inflationary pressures which are within its control. Elements which are strongly influenced by transient, supply shocks need to be excluded. The article proposes four measures of 'Core' inflation which exhibit superiority over the standard measures in tracking the transmission channels of monetary policy, besides being intuitively appealing, timely and robust. By showing that core inflation triggers changes in the non-core components of inflation, the article reinforce the case for monitoring core inflation measures in India.

Inflation is essentially the opportunity cost of sacrificing current spending. A critical factor which impinges upon the cost of holding money is the efficiency with which money is purveyed i.e. the transaction costs of financial intermediation. The article "Profitability of Public Sector Banks-A decomposition Model" by Abhiman Das evaluates inter bank variations of profitability among public sector banks in the context of the financial sector reforms in India with the help of a sequential decomposition model for profitability analysis. A reduction in the burden of raising working funds due to diversification in the banking function and a distinct risk aversion is observed. The article recommends maximising non- interest income by improving the delivery of financial services and efficient management of both sides of the balance sheet in order to economise on transaction costs and strengthen profitability. The process of reform imposes pressure for improvement on the weak banks.

The establishment of a multilateral trading system has come to occupy the centre-stage in the evolution of the international financial architecture. The distinctiveness of the World Trade Organisation (WTO) lies in the degree of accountability it imposes upon member countries. In general, peer pressure and collective action is intended to ensure WTO compatibility for national trade policy regimes. Several areas have been identified in which India's trade policies are in violation of the WTO norms. The review article by Sujan Hajra examines non- tariff aspects of India's foreign trade policies vis-a-vis the WTO with a view to flagging the issues involved and preparing for the millenium round of WTO negotiations at Seattle at the end of this year.
