# High Level Meeting on Regulation of Non-Banking Financial Companies and Unincorporated Bodies

# Functioning in the State of Maharashtra

## August 10, 1999

The Reserve Bank of India, the state level officials and other regulatory agencies have agreed to appoint a small regional coordination committee which will meet at regular periodical intervals to share information and recommend follow up action on regulation of non-banking financial companies and unincorporated bodies functioning in the State of Maharashtra. It was also agreed to hold similar high level meetings of all regulators to improve the quality of supervision over entities engaged in non-banking financial business through public deposit.

The agreement came at a high level meeting on regulation of non-banking financial companies and unincorporated bodies functioning in the state of Maharashtra organised in Mumbai by the Reserve Bank of India today. The meeting was organised to share the expectations of the regulatory framework introduced from January 1997 and appreciate the rationale behind the Reserve Bank seeking active involvement and support of the State Government machinery. The meeting was presided over by Deputy Governor, Shri

S.P. Talwar. The meeting was attended by top police officials of the Economic Offences Wing, Mumbai, Commissioner of Police of Thane, Commissioner of Police of New Mumbai, Central Bureau of Investigations (Economic Offences Wing), State Government official from Home Department, Member of Company Law Board, Registrar of Companies, Deputy Director, Department of Company Affairs Western Regional Office Mumbai and Executive Director, Securities Exchange Board of India. The Deputy Governor explained to the participants the steps initiated by the Reserve Bank in order to consolidate the companies which have already been registered and steps taken in respect of the companies which are weak and defaulting. The Deputy Governor appreciated the Maharashtra State government's initiative in passing the Maharashtra Protection of interests of Depositors (in Financial Establishments) Ordinance, 1999. The Ordinance is designed to protect the interest of the depositors in the State. He sought the help of the other regulatory agencies as well as the state authorities to curb the unauthorised agencies, unincorporated bodies which lure gullible depositors by offering different types of schemes. As they are spread all over the remote parts of the State, State machinery could play a greater role in effectively controlling them, he stated. He also suggested improving market intelligence and sharing the information with other agencies so that there is proper coordination and effective follow up wherever action needed.
