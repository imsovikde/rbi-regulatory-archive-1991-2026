# Non-Y2K compliant banks to face penalties

## January 14, 1999

Banks are at an advanced stage of implementing the Y2K compliance projects in accordance with the remediation framework and compliance deadlines stipulated by the Reserve Bank of India. This was noted by the High Level Steering Group on Y2K issues.

The Reserve Bank of India attaches a great deal of importance to Year 2000 preparations of banks and institutions in the financial sector. To monitor Y2K preparations and to give strategic directions it had, last year, set up a High Level Steering Committee. The Committee is headed by Reserve Bank Deputy Governor, Shri S.P. Talwar, two Executive Directors (S/Shri A. Vasudevan and K. Ahmed), heads of the Reserve Bank's departments exercising supervisory jurisdiction over institutions in the financial sector, National Institute for Bank Management, Indian Banks' Association and representative of banks are other members of the Committee.

The Steering Committee met today to review the progress of banks and institutions in their year 2000 preparations. Shri Rama Krishna, Senior Director, Department of Electronics, Government of India and Shri Sudhir Srivastava, Director, Ministry of Finance, Government of India were also present.

Expressing his concern at the slow pace of the compliance, the Reserve Bank of India Deputy Governor, Shri S.P. Talwar pointed out that those banks whose Y2K compliance status is assessed as unsatisfactory after March 31, 1999 would invite penalties. He also stressed that the focus of compliance efforts should now shift from upgradation, implementation of compliant systems to rigorous testing and contingency planning. The Deputy Governor added that banks should ensure adequate budgets for their year 2000 programmes.

The Department of Banking Supervision, Reserve Bank of India will also meet the public sector, private and foreign banks separately in February to assess each sector's Y2K preparations. It was also decided that the banks will, at the Indian Banks' Association level, meet to exchange their specific experiences with the vendors in making their systems Y2K compliant. The RBI, if necessary, will exhort the vendors to extend their cooperation to the banks. Shri Rama Krishna of the Department of Electronics assured of the Department's full support to the banking sector's efforts in meeting the Y2K compliance deadlines.

The Deputy Governor stated that banks should focus more on developing a contingency plan for operational areas where the customers could be directly affected by the disruption in the systems. As a contingency planning it was decided to keep a printout of all the balances in the branches as on December 29, 1999 to facilitate business continuity across the millennium change and to keep adequate cash in hand to manage the cash transactions in order to meet exigencies.

The RBI will also focus on its own contingency plans for the four MICR clearing centers and its public transactions. The periodicity of the Steering Committee's meetings would now be monthly rather than bimonthly.

<!-- PAGE 2 -->

It may be mentioned that the RBI closely monitors the Y2K preparations of the financial sector through the high level Steering Group. A separate project cell has also been set up, in June 1998, within the Department of Banking Supervision to spearhead the RBI's supervisory initiative on Y2K in respect of the supervised institutions. The Department of Technology looks after the compliance of the internal systems. RBI has articulated a clear supervisory strategy in regard to Y2K comprising both off-site monitoring through monthly reports and on-site verification. A broad remediation framework and compliance deadlines patterned on the internationally suggested framework of Bank for International Settlements have been prescribed.
