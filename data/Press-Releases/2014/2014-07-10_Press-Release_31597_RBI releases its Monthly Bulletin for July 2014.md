## Main findings:

- India’s CAD narrowed sharply to US$ 32.4 billion in 2013-14 from US$ 87.8 billion in 2012-13. CAD as a ratio to GDP declined to 1.7 per cent in 2013-14 from 4.7 per cent in 2012-13.
- On a BoP basis, merchandise exports grew by 3.9 per cent to US$ 318.6 billion in 2013-14 as against a decline of 1.1 per cent in 2012-13. In contrast, merchandise imports at US$ 466.2 billion recorded a decline of 7.2 per cent in 2013-14 as against a marginal increase of 0.5 per cent in 2012-13. The decline was mainly attributed to sharp decline in gold imports by about 46 per cent in 2013-14.
- Notwithstanding a net outflow on account of primary income, net invisible earnings improved in 2013-14, reflecting a rise in net services exports and gross transfer receipts.
- Net inflows of US$ 48.7 billion under capital and financial account, even though significantly lower than the previous year, were more than sufficient to finance CAD.
- There was accretion to India’s foreign exchange reserves to the tune of US$
15.5 billion in 2013-14, considerably higher than that of US$ 3.8 billion in 2012-13.

## 2. Industrial Outlook Survey: 2013-14

*Demand factors show marked improvement in Q4, seasonality helps*

The Reserve Bank of India’s quarterly Industrial Outlook Survey gives insight into the perception of a select group of manufacturing companies about the current business environment and prospects one quarter ahead. Results of the survey are released on the Reserve Bank’s website regularly. Since 2013, an article covering results of four quarterly surveys in a financial year is also being published in the RBI Bulletin. The survey is one among the few forward looking surveys conducted by the Reserve Bank that forms a significant input into the monetary policy.

## Main findings:

- The business sentiments in the manufacturing sector remained weak during the major part of FY:2013-14. The Business Expectation Index (based on assessment) fell below the threshold level of 100, indicating a contraction during Q2 and Q3 of 2013-14. However, in Q4:2013-14 the trend got reversed with the index reaching above the threshold level. The index based on expectation also showed moderation but continued in the growth terrain. Outlook for Q1:2014-15 does not reflect any improvement.
- Weak business sentiments were the result of both inadequate demand and adverse financial situation. However, most of the demand related parameters showed marked improvement in Q4:2013-14, partly reflecting seasonality. Financial condition related parameters reached a low in Q2:2013-14 and made modest recovery thereafter. Cost pressure from raw material and external finance increased during the year. This, together with lower pricing power, impacted the profit margin.
