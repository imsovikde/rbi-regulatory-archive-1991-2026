## September 10, 2014

## RBI releases its Monthly Bulletin for September 2014

The Reserve Bank of India today released the <u>September 2014</u> issue of its monthly Bulletin. The Bulletin includes four special articles: (1) <u>India’s External Debt at end-March</u> 2014; (2) Union Budget 2014-15: An Assessment; **(**3) Consumer Confidence Survey - <u>Q2:2013-14 to Q1:2014-15</u>; and (4) <u>Corporate Investment: Growth in 2013-14 and</u> <u>Prospects for 2014-15</u>.

## 1. India’s External Debt at end-March 2014

## Major Highlights:

- India’s external debt, as at end-March 2014, was placed at US$ 440.6 billion (23.3 per cent of GDP) recording an increase of US$ 31.2 billion (7.6 per cent) over the end-March 2013 level, mainly on account of significant increase in FCNR(B) deposits forming a part of NRI deposits.
- Excluding the valuation change (gain) due to appreciation of US dollar against major international currencies and Indian rupee, the external debt as at end- March 2014 would have increased by US $ 40.6 billion over end-March 2013, instead of US$ 31.2 billion.
- Component-wise, the share of external commercial borrowings continued to be the highest at 33.3 per cent of total external debt, followed by NRI deposits (23.6 per cent) and short term debt (20.3 per cent).
- The short-term debt as at end-March 2014 declined by US$ 7.5 billion (7.7 per cent) over the previous year level.
- The share of short-term debt in total debt, by original maturity, was 20.3 per cent. Based on residual maturity, short-term debt accounted for 39.6 per cent of the total external debt as at end-March 2014. Outstanding NRI deposits accounted for 31.4 per cent of short-term external debt based on residual maturity.
- The ratio of short-term debt (residual maturity) to foreign exchange reserves at
57.4 per cent at end- March 2014 was lower compared to 59 per cent as at end- March 2013.
- The debt service ratio remained stable at 5.9 per cent during 2013-14.
- India’s foreign exchange reserves provided a cover of 69 per cent to the external debt stock at the end of March 2014 as compared with 71.3 per cent at end- March 2013.
- The US dollar denominated debt accounted for 61.8 per cent of the total external debt stock as at end-March 2014 followed by that in Indian rupee (21.1 per cent) and SDR (6.9 per cent).

## 2. Union Budget 2014-15: An Assessment

This article, based on the Union Budget 2014-15 presented to the Parliament on July 10, 2014, presents the key features of the Budget and makes an assessment of the likely impact of various budgetary measures on fiscal and overall macroeconomic situation in 2014-15.

## Major Highlights:

- Gross fiscal deficit (GFD), as percentage of GDP in the provisional accounts for 2013-14 was 4.5 per cent, lower than the revised estimates and budget estimates for the year. Reduction in revenue deficit through cutbacks in plan and non-plan revenue expenditure coupled with higher non-debt capital receipts and lower capital expenditure resulted in a marginally lower GFD-GDP ratio compared to revised estimates of 4.6 per cent.
- The Budget estimates for 2014-15 indicate a continuation of the process of fiscal consolidation, with all key deficit indicators relative to GDP set to decline. The Budget estimates the GFD-GDP ratio to decline to 4.1 per cent in 2014-15 (BE). In the medium-term, the GFD-GDP ratio is expected to decline to 3 per cent by 2016-17.
- The envisaged reduction in GFD in 2014-15 is to be achieved through enhanced non-debt receipts-GDP ratio which would more than offset a marginal increase in total expenditure-GDP ratio. Non-debt receipts are budgeted to increase by 18.6 per cent in 2014-15 (15.8 per cent in 2013-14), with significant increases to be recorded in tax revenue and disinvestment receipts.
- The growth in personal income tax collections will be adversely affected, following exemption proposals announced in the budget. The indirect taxes, however, are budgeted to grow at a faster rate in 2014-15 as compared to the previous year, mainly on the assumption of a revival in economic growth. Service tax collections are estimated to grow at 31 per cent in 2014-15 (BE) over 2013-14 (RE).
- Total subsidy expenditure is budgeted to be contained at 2 per cent of GDP through reduction in provision for petroleum subsidy.
- Capital expenditure as a per cent of GDP is budgeted to increase marginally to
1.8 per cent in 2014-15, still below 2 per cent attained in 2010-11.
## 3. Consumer Confidence Survey-Q2:2013-14 to Q1:2014-15

The Consumer Confidence Survey provides an assessment of around 5,400 respondents’ perception spread across six metropolitan cities *viz*., Bengaluru, Chennai, Hyderabad, Kolkata, Mumbai and New Delhi. The survey captures qualitative information on a 3 point scale *viz*., improve, remain same, or worsen on questions pertaining to economic conditions, household circumstances, income, spending, prices and employment prospects. The assessments are analysed in two parts, *viz.*, current situation as compared with a year ago and expectations for a year ahead. This article provides salient findings based on the last four rounds (Q2:2013-14 to Q1:2014-15) of the survey. The survey results reflect views of the respondents and are not necessarily shared by the Reserve Bank of India.

## Main Findings:

- Current Situation Index recovered slowly in Q1:2014-15 from the low levels observed in Q2:2013-14. There has been significant improvement in Future Expectations Index due to increase in the positive perceptions on almost all parameters during the last four rounds of the survey. However, the increase has not been uniform across all indicators.

<!-- PAGE 3 -->

- The optimism regarding future expectations on economic conditions, household circumstances and income has consistently been better than the views held on the current situation on these parameters.
- ‘Cost of consumer goods’, ‘cost of services’ and ‘income’ were observed to be the major factors that have influenced the perceptions on current spending.
- More than three fourths of the respondents expected the prices to increase over the next year. Out of these, more than 80 per cent expected inflation to rise next year.
- The respondents had a more optimistic outlook regarding employment scenario.
## 4. Corporate Investment: Growth in 2013-14 and Prospects for 2014-15

The article captures capital expenditure (investment in fixed assets) intentions of the companies in private and joint business sector, based on their financing details, in order to assess broadly the short-term changes in business sentiment. The estimate of capital investment likely to be incurred during the current financial year is based on time phasing details of the investment intentions of the companies which have raised funds from banks/FIs or through ECBs/FCCBs and domestic equity issues. Capital expenditure envisaged from pipeline projects is also estimated for the year 2014-15.

## Main Findings:

- The study covers 481 projects with the envisaged project cost of `1,340 billion receiving sanction for financial assistance from banks/FIs during 2013-14. It also covers 563 projects contracting ECBs/FCCBs to the tune of `803 billion and 21 projects raising resources through equity issues of `5 billion. Thus, the total cost of projects receiving finance through these three routes in 2013-14 aggregated `2,148 billion as against `2,634 billion in 2012-13.
