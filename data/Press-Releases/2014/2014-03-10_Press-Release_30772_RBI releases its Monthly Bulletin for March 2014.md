## March 10, 2014

## RBI releases its Monthly Bulletin for March 2014

The Reserve Bank of India today released the <u>March 2014</u> issue of its monthly Bulletin. The Bulletin includes three special articles: (1) Developments in India's Balance of Payments during Second Quarter (July-September) of 2013-14; (2) Survey on International Trade in Banking Services: 2012-13; and (3) Survey on Computer Software & Information Technology Enabled Services Exports: 2012-13.

## 1. <u>Developments in India's Balance of Payments during Second Quarter(July-September) of 2013-14</u>

This article provides details on developments in India’s balance of payments during July-September 2013 (Q2 of 2013-14).

## Main Findings:

India’s current account deficit (CAD) recorded a steep decline in Q2 of 2013-14 owing to a significant contraction of trade deficit. Net capital inflows, however, slowed down significantly, triggered by an indication by the US Fed in May 2013 on the probable tapering of its quantitative easing programme. Slowdown in net capital inflows and a downward pressure on the exchange rate led to a drawdown of foreign exchange reserves during the quarter.

- India’s CAD narrowed sharply to US$ 5.2 billion in Q2 of 2013-14 from US$ 21 billion in Q2 of 2012-13, also much lower than the CAD of US$ 21.8 billion in Q1 of 2013-14. As a per cent of GDP, CAD declined to 1.2 per cent in Q2 of 2013-14 from 5 per cent in Q2 of 2012-13 (4.9 per cent in Q1 of 2012-13).
- On a BoP basis, merchandise exports increased by 11.9 per cent to US$ 81.2 billion in Q2 of 2013-14. In contrast, merchandise imports at US$ 114.5 billion, recorded a decline of 4.8 per cent in Q2 of 2013-14, mainly attributed to a moderation in gold imports by about 65 per cent in Q2 of 2013-14.
- Notwithstanding a net outflow on account of primary income, net invisible earnings improved in Q2 of 2013-14, reflecting a rise in net services exports and increase in gross transfer receipts.
- There was a net outflow under financial account (excluding foreign exchange reserves) of US$ 5.3 billion in Q2 of 2013-14 as against a net inflow of US$ 20.8 billion in Q2 of 2012-13.
- Despite a low CAD, there was a drawdown of foreign exchange reserves of US$
10.4 billion in Q2 of 2013-14 as compared to that of US$ 0.2 billion in Q2 of 2012- 13, as net capital inflows moderated sharply during the quarter.

## 2. <u>Survey on International Trade in Banking Services: 2012-13</u>

The Reserve Bank’s annual survey on International Trade in Banking Services (ITBS) is intended to provide information on ITBS for India in respect of the branches/subsidiaries of Indian banks operating abroad and foreign banks operating in India. Consistent and comparable data are captured on financial auxiliaries’ services rendered by the banks based on explicit/ implicit fee /commission charged to customers. The 2012-13 survey round covered 170 overseas branches, 184 overseas subsidiaries of Indian Banks and 316 branches of foreign banks operating in India.

The survey results reveal that, the consolidated balance sheet of overseas branches of Indian banks, which moderated after the global financial crisis, recovered in subsequent years whereas that of foreign banks operating in India continue to grow at a relatively moderate pace. Total fee income generated by 170 branches of Indian banks operating outside India increased from ` 68.0 billion in 2011-12 to ` 93.5 billion in 2012- 13 whereas, in case of foreign banks operating in India, total fee income generated by 316 branches declined from ` 94.3 billion in 2011-12 to ` 74.5 billion in 2012-13. Bahrain, Belgium, Hong Kong, Japan, Singapore, Sri Lanka, UAE, UK and USA were the major countries where banking services were provided by overseas branches of Indian banks. Overseas subsidiaries of Indian banks were mainly present in Botswana, Canada, Russia and United Kingdom.

The data relating to the results of Survey on International Trade in Banking Services: 2012-13 was earlier released on the RBI website vide <u>Press release No. 1647</u> on February 17, 2014.

## 3. <u>Survey on Computer Software & Information Technology Enabled Services Exports: 2012-13</u>

The article on Survey on Computer Software & Information Technology Enabled Services Exports: 2012-13 presents information on export of software services as per the activity, type of services (on-site/off-site) and country of destination along with the four modes of supply (cross-border supply, consumption abroad, commercial presence and presence of natural person). The current round of survey covered 737 companies which includes most of the large companies, covering 75.4 per cent of the total estimated software exports during 2012-13.

India’s export of software services during 2012-13 stood at ` 3,405.2 billion (US$

62.6 billion), of which computer services exports accounted for 71.9 per cent. The global software export of India including the services rendered by foreign affiliates established abroad was estimated at ` 3,759 billion (US$ 69.1 billion). Delivery of software services through on-site mode exhibited continuous downward trend following global financial crisis. USA continued to remain the top destination for software exports and US dollar remained the major invoice currency for software exports during 2012-13. Cross-border supply continued to be the dominant mode of software services exports. The data relating to the results of Survey on Computer Software & Information Technology Enabled Services Exports: 2012-13 was earlier released on the RBI website vide <u>Press release No. 1648</u> on February 17, 2014.
