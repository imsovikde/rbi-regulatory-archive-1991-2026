## January 24, 2004

## Think Innovatively:

## RBI Governor tells State Finance Secretaries

"Think innovatively to find solutions and do not take an ideological approach while resolving any issues.” This was the advice of Dr. Y.V. Reddy, Governor, Reserve Bank of India to the State Finance Secretaries. He said that given the challenges, the States have managed well and added that the successive State Finance Secretaries Conferences had succeeded in building bridges between States, Centre, Reserve Bank, Credit Rating Agencies and the market. He was addressing a two-day Conference of State Finance Secretaries organised by the Reserve Bank of India, Mumbai. The conference was held on January 23 and 24, 2004.

The State Finance Secretaries’ Conference is a bi-annual event organised by the Reserve Bank of India. It provides a forum to the State Finance Secretaries to interact freely among themselves as also with the Central Government, the Comptroller and Auditor General of India, Controller General of Accounts and the Planning Commission th while the Reserve Bank provides technical assistance. This was the 13 Conference of the State Finance Secretaries. Dr. Rakesh Mohan, Deputy Governor, chaired the conference. Smt. Aruna Makhan, Controller General of Accounts, Shri K.G. Mahalingam, Director General, Comptroller General of Accounts, Dr. N.J. Kurian, Adviser (Planning Commission), Shri S.C. Garg, Joint Secretary, Ministry of Finance and Finance Secretaries from 20 States attended the conference.

In his remarks, Dr. Y.V. Reddy, Governor, Reserve Bank of India expressed satisfaction at the fact that the State Finance Secretaries were at the forefront of the State level reforms process. The Constitution of India provided for adequate safeguards against crisis in the centre-state relationship, he stated and added that mismanagement in the fiscal area has the potential to spill-over to other areas and cause crises. He was, therefore, happy that the State finance secretaries played an active role in the state level fiscal reforms. He, however, urged them to think innovatively and not take an ideological stand on the issues involved in reforms.

The experience in market borrowing programme of state governments was indicative of the market perception regarding the fiscal management of the states, according to him. Acknowledging the diversities of the states, the Governor advocated that the Model Fiscal Responsibility Bill should be adapted to diverse circumstances and

should not be adopted merely in letter. "There are no short-term gains and it would not serve much purpose if the Bill is adopted in letter but not in spirit", he stated.

Referring to the Report on Pension Reforms presented by a Group of Finance Secretaries, the Governor acknowledged that the problem of future liabilities of pension was huge and not one that can be resolved overnight. He suggested that the Finance Secretaries should rather first aim at creating appropriate institutional structure so that the problem can be handled smoothly in the medium-term.

In his opening remarks, Dr Rakesh Mohan, Deputy Governor, highlighted some of the positive achievements of the States during the current financial year, such as, reduced recourse to Ways and Means Advances and Overdraft with the Reserve Bank, reduced fiscal concessions, reduction in the overdue interest position of some states, improving situation on relative share of capital expenditure, and the like. Dr Mohan also flagged major issues relating to the need for augmenting States efforts at containing expenditure and enhancing revenue with both tax and non-tax measures to avert the fiscal deterioration of State governments. Referring to the experience of the market borrowings programme of the States, he reiterated the need to avoid defaults on state guaranteed bonds.

In the context of restructuring of loans of State Governments, it was clarified that rescheduling/ renegotiation in the SLR (Statutory Liquidity Ratio) bonds would undermine the sovereign status of these bonds. On the issue of bonds issued by state financial corporations (SFCs), it was suggested that the States could consider swapping the high cost debt of SFCs if they so wished.

Other operational details discussed related to, among others, preparation of database on State Government guarantees, details on guarantees outstanding, maturity profile of loans and advances to be furnished by the State Governments to the Reserve Bank.

Decisions were taken in regard to (i) meeting to be held between the Government and institutions, such as, HUDCO, PFC, REC, etc., to impress upon them the need to comply with the instructions regarding guarantees, (ii) A workshop to be conducted to impart risk evaluation skills to State Government officials to monitor and to mitigate the risks arising out of devolvement of guarantees, (iii) A Working Group to be constituted to examine the issues relating to credit enhancement in infrastructure projects. The Group would have representations from Andhra Pradesh, Assam, Rajasthan, Public Infrastructure Development Board, Punjab and Government of India and (v) Another Working Group to be constituted with representations from Uttaranchal, Meghalaya, Karnataka, Chhattisgarh, Maharashtra and RBI departments, viz., Central Accounts Section, Nagpur, Department of Information Technology and Department of Government and Bank Accounts to examine the issues relating to uniform application software for treasury operations.

The Report of the Group to Study the Pension Liabilities of the State Governments (chaired by Shri B K Bhattacharya) was presented to the Finance Secretaries. In view of the importance of the issue, it was decided that the report should be put in the public domain so that further public discussion can take place on the subject.

A panel discussion on “Financing Municipal Bonds-Issues and Prospects” was also held on January 23, 2004. The discussants comprised Shri Rajivan Krishnaswamy, Managing Director, Tamil Nadu Urban Development Fund, Shri S.C.Garg, Joint Secretary, Ministry of Finance, Government of India, Shri E.S.Jayaraman, Managing Director, Credit Analysis and Research Ltd. Shri K.P.Krishnan, Managing Director, Karnataka Urban Infrastructure Development and Finance Corporation and Shri Baman Mehta, Darashaw and Co. The panel was chaired by Smt. Shyamala Gopinath, Executive Director, Reserve Bank of India. The discussants noted that for the viability of projects it is essential that the debt is contracted at the least possible cost. The advantages of financing through pooled projects *vis a vis* the traditional methods of grants and guarantees, collection of upfront charges from potential users, employment of professional techniques for maintenance and upkeep of projects as also better cash utilisation methods were brought out. Interactions focussed on issues relating to, among others, taxation, collection of user charges, and the role of the rating agencies in broad basing the investor base. The experience with regard to floatation of municipal bonds for the cities of Ahmedabad, Ludhiana, Bangalore, Nasik, Madurai, Hyderabad and Indore was cited.

On the second day of the conference, a Workshop on Restructuring and Disinvestment of State Level Public Sector Undertakings was arranged for the State Finance Secretaries. Dr. R.H. Patil, Chairman, Disinvestment Commission, Government of India delivered the keynote address. Shri Dhirendra Singh, Secretary, Ministry of Divestment, Government of India chaired the inter-active session. In his address, Dr. Patil explained the Commission's approach to disinvestments. Competition and not ownership was the key to efficiency in an organisation, he pointed out. Given the right policy framework and full freedom to the management, public and private sectors can perform better. Detailing the Commission’s recommendations, Dr. Patil stated that disinvestments should be through initial public offering if in its opinion the Government had a role to play in the sector in which the public sector undertaking was functioning. If not, it would recommend a strategic disinvestment. The key to successful disinvestments, in Dr. Patil’s view, was transparency. Shri Dhirendra Singh, Secretary, Ministry of Divestment, Government of India also stressed on transparency and due diligence in procedures while disinvesting. The Workshop included presentations on the subject by the Punjab and Maharashtra Governments as also by the State Bank of India, ICICI Bank Ltd. and CRISIL.
