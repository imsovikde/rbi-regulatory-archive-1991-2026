**India’s Bond Markets have Potential to be the Largest and the Most Efficient: RBI Deputy Governor**

The Government securities (G-sec) market in India is now well-developed with institutions, intermediaries, technology, processes and such other infrastructure and has laid down the foundations for the development of a corporate debt market. With the largeness of the country and its financial markets, India can well be the largest and the most efficient bond market in Asia in the next decade. The efforts that have gone into achieving the level of development in the G-sec market, however, only illustrate the effort that the regulators, the market participants and the self regulatory organisations will have to undertake to develop the corporate debt market. This was the undertone of the Reserve Bank of India’s Deputy Governor, Dr. Rakesh Mohan’s remarks at the Fifth FIMMDA-PDAI Annual Conference held in Dubai on March 20, 2004.

Paying rich tributes to Dr. Y V Reddy, Governor, Reserve Bank of India, Dr. Rakesh Mohan said that it was Dr. Reddy who had laid down the road map for the development of the G-sec market in India at a FIMMDA-PDAI conference two years back at Bangkok. The clarity and practicality of the road map were evident in the progress report that he was presenting today which showed a high level of achievement. Dr. Rakesh Mohan then gave a detailed account of the developments in the G-sec market to demonstrate that the market now had the necessary infrastructure, institutions and instruments as also processes well in place. However, there was scope for further improvement if India’s G-sec market is to become one of the most efficient bond markets in the world and added that the Reserve Bank will continue with further consolidation of the government debt market.

He said that the repo market would be widened to include corporate entities to participate with adequate safeguards. He also indicated that the Reserve Bank will soon issue guidelines on the sale of securities purchased previously to take care of price risk and rollover of repos, DVP III mode of settlement and making the call money market exclusively for banks as announced in the Monetary Policy of November 2003. The Reserve Bank was also actively considering the index linked bonds. The role of the Primary Dealers would be enlarged and the issue of granting limited exclusivity in primary

issuances of G-secs to them was also under consideration of the Reserve Bank. The privileges for the Primary Dealers, however, would come with greater responsibility on them for market making and retailing of G-secs. He further stated that the CCIL had stabilised and was expected to further develop the repo market with corporate participation. Stating that the implementation of the RTGS was critically poised to start soon, the Deputy Governor underlined the importance of an anonymous order driven trading system on NDS to usher in a more efficient and transparent trading process.

While the G-sec markets were now fairly well-developed, the corporate debt markets needed to go a long way so that India can have a well-integrated financial market. An integrated financial market was necessary for efficient transmission of monetary policy now that it operated on indirect instruments, the Deputy Governor pointed out. Fortunately, the foundations for developing an efficient corporate debt market were already laid as the necessary infrastructure and institutions that have been created for the G-sec market are already available. The regulators have also taken the first initiative in this regard with SEBI announcing its guidelines on trading and listing requirements for corporate debt and the RBI asking banks to invest only in listed and rated corporate paper on a private placement basis.

Dwelling at some length on the need for development of a healthy and efficient corporate debt market, the Deputy Governor stated that with the passing of the Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management Act, the Government would be required to reduce its deficit which in turn will result in reduced market borrowing programme. The reduced availability of G-secs will force banks to look for other investment opportunities. Further, the economy being on a high growth path would create higher demand for funds, especially in the infrastructure. Corporates looking at raising funds from the capital markets would also require them to raise the balancing debt. With the term lending from financial institutions on the decline, the corporate funding needs, especially of infrastructure, would necessarily have to be met by the bond market. Terming these as motivating factors for development of corporate debt market, the Deputy Governor added that given a large pool of well-informed urban population with 35 cities having 10 million population, he also saw a huge potential in this market for new issuers and investors.

However, sounding a note of caution, the Deputy Governor stated that while the development of corporate debt market was an opportunity, the experience of developing the G-sec market has shown that it is a long drawn out and an arduous effort which requires cooperative and collaborative efforts from not only the regulators but also from the market participants and self regulatory organisations. Development of G-sec market was in a way simple as the Reserve Bank, apart from being the regulator of financial markets, was also the issuer of the paper. The players – both issuers and investors-in the corporate debt market would be varied. It was therefore important for the regulators to focus

attention on protecting investors’ interest by developing processes of clearing and settlement that would ensure risk free environment. India has the potential for developing into the largest and the most efficient bond market, the Deputy Governor stated and hoped that the regulators and market participants together would work hard to making it happen.
