The revised framework also enables banks/supervisors to select approaches that are most appropriate for their operations and financial markets. The framework is expected to promote adoption of stronger risk management practices in banks.

The Basel II framework envisages a three-pillar approach. Pillar I prescribes the minimum capital requirement. Pillar II comprises supervisory review that not only ensures that banks have adequate capital to support all the risks in their business, but also encourages banks to develop and use better risk management techniques in monitoring and managing their risks. Pillar III complements Pillar I and II in encouraging market discipline by developing a set of disclosure requirements that allow market participants to assess key information on the scope of application, capital, risk exposures, risk assessment process and hence the capital adequacy of a bank.

Since the Standardised Approach is based on banks’ internal assessments, the potential for more risk sensitive capital requirements is substantial. In India, until the financial year ended March 2004, banks were required to maintain capital for credit risk in the lines of Basel I. These banks were also required to maintain capital charge for market risks by way of additional risk weights for their investment portfolio, open gold and forex positions and investment fluctuation reserve. With effect from the current financial year, banks in India will be maintaining capital for credit risk and market risks on the lines of Basel I. Thus capital charge for operational risk will be a new feature under Basel II.

The Seminar, over two days, will discuss the various concepts of the Basel II framework and their implications on the banks in India.
