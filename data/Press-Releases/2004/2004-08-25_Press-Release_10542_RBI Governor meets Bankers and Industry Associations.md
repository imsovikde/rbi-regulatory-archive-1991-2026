rising rapidly, as is the case now. Any such operation should be only to provide comfort of liquidity at the market determined rates consistent with yield curve. Overall, the market participants felt that there is considerable merit in continuing to give weight to stability at this juncture to meet not only the current concerns in bond and money markets, but also to avoid the prospects of inducing stagflation in as much as a significant reason for current hike in WPI is due to supply shock. It was also indicated that stability in government securities market will have a positive impact on corporate bond markets also.

The suggestions for medium term also covered several areas. Some of the participants focussed on the role of Wholesale (or Producer, as is called in other countries) Price Index, Consumer Price Index and that of asset prices in influencing the inflationary expectations and policy response and suggested that they be addressed in addition to the measurement and divergence issues of the indices currently under examination in India. Many participants urged that serious consideration be given to permitting short selling, futures and when-issued market as medium term measures to improve government securities market. The issue of the tightening of provisioning norms for state- guaranteed loans was raised by bankers who requested that they be deferred beyond one year to give time to state governments and banks to adjust to the proposed norms. More generally it was noted that for several decades, interest rates were steady and some times in double digits in India. In recent years, there has been a down trend which benefited all. It is but natural for the markets to have interest-rate cycles. There was a feeling that the market participants and others need to appreciate and be prepared for such cycles. However, since this is first time in India, some measures of “healing touch” by RBI at operational level would be appropriate, without necessarily linking all such measures to overall stance of monetary policy.

The Governor explained that the Reserve Bank, like other central banks, is closely monitoring the developments. He noted the sentiments of the participants in favour of minimising volatility in domestic financial markets, though the uncertainties in global factors have turned more adverse since the announcement of the Annual Policy in May 2004. He assured, however, that on suggestions relating to operational aspects, there would be consideration in RBI on a priority basis. More intense consultations on medium term issues flagged in the meeting will be held with the respective Advisory Committees of RBI in which market participants are also represented.

The Governor reiterated the importance accorded to the primary objective of maintaining price stability and consequently containing inflationary expectations, while giving due consideration to growth in output and employment. He added that the progress made in moderating inflationary expectations would have to be maintained.
