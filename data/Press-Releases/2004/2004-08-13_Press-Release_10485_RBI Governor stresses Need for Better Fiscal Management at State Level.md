# RBI Governor stresses Need for Better Fiscal Management at State Level

<!-- PAGE 1 -->

` PRESS RELEASE PRESS RELATIONS DIVISION, Central Office, Post Box 406, Mumbai 400001 Phone: 2266 0502 Fax: 2266 0358,  2270 3279 RESERVE BANK OF INDIA www.rbi.org.in www.rbi.org.in\hindi e-mail: helpprd@rbi.org.in August 13, 2004 RBI Governor stresses Need for Better  Fiscal Management at State Level The 14th Conference of the State Finance Secretaries was held in the Reserve Bank of India today. Finance Secretaries of 24 states participated. Senior officials from Ministry of Finance, Government of India and representatives from Comptroller and Auditor General attended the Conference. Dr. Rakesh Mohan, Deputy Governor, Reserve Bank of India chaired the Conference. Smt. Shyamala Gopinath, Executive Director, Reserve Bank of India and other senior officials of the Reserve Bank were present. In his inaugural interaction with the State Finance Secretaries, Dr. Y.V. Reddy, Governor, Reserve Bank of India reiterated the need for greater fiscal management at the state level to ensure completion of the approved borrowings of the state governments. Dwelling on the subject of inflation and interest rates the Governor stated that the Reserve Bank will continue to carefully monitor the developments in the context of international and domestic factors. Talking about improving the health of the cooperative banks, the Governor felt that the dual control regime is a constraint of effective regulation and welcomed the recent initiatives taken by the Government of India. He urged the finance secretaries to impress upon their governments the critical role of state governments, especially through the regional rural banks in enhancing credit delivery to agriculture. The Reserve Bank has been, since 1997, organising the State Finance Secretaries' Conference twice every year. The Conference all through has provided a very useful forum for interactions among State Finance Secretaries, senior officials of Government of India, Planning Commission, Comptroller and Auditor General, CGA and the Reserve Bank of India. The State Finance Secretaries play a key role in the development process of their respective States and in striving to achieve transparency, stability, fairness and efficiency in the conduct of fiscal policies. The deliberations in the past Conferences have served to arrive at a consensus on various issues in furtherance of better financial management and fiscal prudence in the States. Today's Conference aimed to take this consultative process further. Important issues discussed in today's Conference included recent experience in increased market borrowings by State Governments, enhancing the liquidity of State Government securities, dimensions of debt sustainability of States, credit enhancement for infrastructure financing at the State level, banking arrangements for implementation of VAT, computerisation of loans to states

given by the Central Government, financial position of State and District Central Co-operative Banks and other procedural issues relating to Government transactions and currency operations. Keeping in view the need for rule based fiscal policy for achieving fiscal sustainability and ensuring higher degree of transparency and disclosure in state budgets, a draft model fiscal responsibility legislation at the state level was also discussed. The draft model legislation was prepared by a Working Group comprising select State Finance Secretaries, a representative each from the Government of India and the Reserve Bank of India. It was also decided in the Conference to form two Working Groups with representatives from select State Government on

(i) improving secondary market liquidity of State Development Loans and (ii) methodology of compilation of data on various types of debt liabilities of the States. Dr. Rakesh Mohan, Deputy Governor, in his concluding remarks hoped that the state finance secretaries would continue to focus on the issue of debt sustainability with a long term perspective and endeavour to find robust solutions.
