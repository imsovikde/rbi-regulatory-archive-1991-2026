In exercise of the powers conferred by Sections 45J, 45JA, 45K, 45L, 45M, and 45MA of the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934, and all other provisions / laws enabling the Reserve Bank of India (‘RBI’) in this regard, the RBI being satisfied that it is necessary and expedient in the public interest to do so, hereby issues the Directions hereinafter specified. **Preamble:** The Master Direction (MD) provides applicable guidelines as well as guidance related to climate finance and management of climate change risks. The objective is to enable Non-Banking Financial Companies to carry out comprehensive assessment of climate change risks, integrate climate change risk considerations into their extant risk management frameworks and structures and optimise flow of credit to green activities / projects overcoming greenwashing challenges, protecting interest of the depositors and thereby aiding customers to achieve their sustainability agenda. **Chapter I – Preliminary**

**A. Short Title and Commencement**
1. These Directions shall be called the Reserve Bank of India (Non-Banking Financial Companies – Climate Finance and Management of Climate Change Risks) Directions, 2025.
2. These Directions shall come into force with immediate effect.
## B. Applicability

3. These Directions shall be applicable to deposit taking Non-Banking Financial Companies registered with the Reserve Bank of India under clause (5) of Section 45IA of the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934 (hereinafter collectively referred to as ‘NBFCs’ and individually as an ‘NBFC’). The deposit taking NBFCs shall be classified under the Middle Layer and above, in accordance with the <u>Reserve Bank</u> <u>of India (Non-Banking Financial Companies – Registration, Exemptions and</u> <u>Framework for Scale Based Regulation) Guidelines, 2025</u>. *Notwithstanding paragraph 3 above, Housing Finance Companies shall continue to* *be governed by the extant directions applicable to them, as amended from time to* *time.*

<!-- PAGE 3 -->

## C. Definitions

4. In these directions, unless the context states otherwise, the terms herein shall bear the meanings assigned to them below:
(i) ‘green activities / projects’ means activities / projects meeting the requirements prescribed in paragraph 13 of these Directions; (ii) ‘green deposit’ means an interest-bearing deposit, received by an NBFC for a fixed period and the proceeds of which are earmarked for allocation towards green finance;
(iii) ‘green finance’ means lending to and / or investing in green activities / projects meeting the requirements prescribed in paragraph 13 of these Directions. Such lending contributes to climate risk mitigation, climate adaptation and resilience, and other climate-related or environmental objectives-including biodiversity management and nature-based solution; (iv) ‘greenwashing’ means the practice of marketing products / services as green, when in fact they do not meet requirements to be defined as green activities / projects.

5. All other expressions unless defined herein shall have the same meaning as have been assigned to them under The Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934 or The Banking Regulation Act, 1949 or any statutory modification or re-enactment thereto, or Glossary of terms published by the RBI or as used in commercial parlance, as the case may be.
## Chapter II – Role of the Board

6. An NBFC desirous of raising green deposits shall put in place comprehensive Board-approved policies on Green Deposits and Financing Framework, the coverage of which are indicated in paragraph 8 and paragraph 10 respectively of these Directions.
7. The Board shall be responsible for reviewing the NBFC’s green deposit initiatives through a review report to be placed in accordance with the provisions specified in paragraph 18 of these Directions.

## Chapter III – Framework for acceptance of Green Deposits

## A. Policy

8. The Board-approved policy on green deposits of an NBFC shall lay down all aspects, in detail, for the issuance and allocation of green deposits. A copy of the policy on ‘Green Deposits’ shall be made available on the website of the NBFC. An NBFC may finance any green activities / projects irrespective of raising green deposits for the same. However, an NBFC shall not finance green activities / projects first and raise green deposits thereafter for the same.
**B.** Denomination, interest rates and tenor of deposits
9. An NBFC shall issue green deposits as cumulative / non-cumulative deposits. On maturity, the green deposits shall be renewed or withdrawn at the option of the depositor. The green deposits shall only be denominated in Indian Rupees and not in any foreign currency. The tenor, size, interest rate, and other terms and conditions as defined in the <u>Reserve Bank of India (Non-Banking Financial Companies-</u> <u>Acceptance of Public Deposits) Directions, 2025</u>, as amended from time to time, shall also be applicable to green deposits *mutatis mutandis*. *Explanation:*
(i) The extant guidelines enumerated above do not permit an NBFC to offer differential rate of interest on green deposits. (ii) An NBFC shall pay interest on green deposits to its customers as per agreed terms and conditions and aforesaid directions irrespective of allocation / utilisation of proceeds. (iii) With respect to premature withdrawal of green deposits, while there is no restriction, an NBFC shall adhere to the aforesaid directions. Further, premature withdrawal shall not have any bearing on the activities / projects undertaken using the proceeds of green deposits.
## C. Financing Framework

10. The Board-approved Financing Framework (FF) put in place by an NBFC shall cover, *inter-alia*, the following for effective allocation of green deposits:

<!-- PAGE 5 -->

(i) the eligible green activities / projects that could be financed out of proceeds raised through the green deposits (as permitted in paragraph 13 of these Directions); *Explanation:* Investment by an NBFC in Sovereign Green Bonds (SGrBs) are covered under the framework. (ii) the process for project evaluation and selection by an NBFC including identifying the projects fit for lending / investing within the eligible categories, monitoring and validating all the related / required information provided by the borrower;
(iii) the allocation of proceeds of green deposits and its reporting and disclosures, Third-Party Verification / Assurance, and Impact Assessment; and (iv) the particulars of the temporary allocation (which would only be in High Quality Liquid Assets without any haircut as defined in <u>Reserve Bank of India</u> <u>(Non-Banking Financial Companies-Asset Liability Management) Directions,</u> <u>2025</u>, up to a maximum original tenure of one year) of green deposit proceeds, pending their allocation to the eligible activities / projects. *Explanation:*

(a) An NBFC can temporarily park proceeds of green deposits, pending allocation towards eligible green activities / projects, in liquid instruments with maximum maturity of one year.
(b) While the framework does not envisage any penalty for non-allocation of proceeds towards eligible green activities / projects, it shall be subject to supervisory review.
11. **External Review** – An NBFC shall arrange to carry out an external review of their FF.
12. Before the implementation of FF, a copy of the FF shall be made available on the website of the NBFC along with the opinion of external reviewer.

## E. Third-Party Verification / Assurance

14. The allocation of funds raised through green deposits by an NBFC during a financial year shall be subject to an annual independent third-party verification / assurance. The third-party verification / assurance would not absolve the NBFC of its responsibility regarding the end-use of funds, for which it shall follow the laid down procedures of internal checks and balances as in the case of other loans. The terms and conditions to be additionally fulfilled by the borrowers to meet the requirements of the framework, as laid out in paragraph 10 to paragraph 13 of these Directions, are the additional check points which have to be adhered to by the NBFC for ensuring the end-use of funds. *Explanation:* An NBFC can engage with any appropriate and reputed domestic / international agency for external review of the FF, third-party verification / assurance, and impact assessment of the green activities / projects.
15. The third-party verification / assurance report shall, at the minimum, cover the following aspects:

<!-- PAGE 8 -->

(i) Use of the proceeds by the NBFC should be in accordance with the eligible green activities / projects. It shall monitor the end-use of funds allocated against the deposits raised. (ii) Policies and internal controls including, *inter-alia*, project evaluation and selection, management of proceeds, validation of all the related / required information provided by the borrower to the NBFC, and reporting and disclosures.
## F. Impact Assessment

16. An NBFC shall, with the assistance of external firms, assess annually the impact associated of the funds lent for or invested in green finance activities / projects during a financial year through an Impact Assessment Report. An illustrative list of impact indicators is tabulated below. In case an NBFC is unable to quantify the impact of their lending / investment, it shall disclose, at the minimum, the reasons, the difficulties encountered, and the time-bound plans to address the same.

## Chapter IV-Repeal and other provisions

## A. Repeal and saving

20. With the issue of these Directions, the existing Directions, instructions, and guidelines relating to framework for acceptance of green deposits as applicable to Non-Banking Financial Companies stand repealed, as communicated vide notification dated XX, 2025. The Directions, instructions, and guidelines repealed prior to the issuance of these Directions shall continue to remain repealed.
21. Notwithstanding such repeal, any action taken or purported to have been taken, or initiated under the repealed Directions, instructions, or guidelines shall continue to be governed by the provisions thereof. All approvals or acknowledgments granted under these repealed lists shall be deemed as governed by these Directions.
## B. Application of other laws not barred

22. The provisions of these Directions shall be in addition to, and not in derogation of the provisions of any other laws, rules, regulations, or directions, for the time being in force.
## C. Interpretations

23. For the purpose of giving effect to the provisions of these Directions or in order to remove any difficulties in the application or interpretation of the provisions of these Directions, the RBI may, if it considers necessary, issue necessary clarifications in respect of any matter covered herein and the interpretation of any provision of these Directions given by the RBI shall be final and binding.
